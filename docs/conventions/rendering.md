# The rendering

`src/rendering/` draws the slope with PixiJS ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture): the ground, the ropes, the ladders and every climber as the slope physics has them, behind and in front of them the parallax layers, and the snow. `src/page/` puts the game together on the page: the start's choice, alone or in a room, the expedition, its loop, the camera, the scene and the controls. Both follow [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules and Objects; these are their own rules. How the picture looks and how fast it's drawn follow the design guidelines too: [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) and [../guidelines/performance.md](../guidelines/performance.md).

- **The rendering reads the simulation, and holds no game state.** `SlopeScene` is given a `SceneWorld`, the physics, the hazards, the mountain's clock, the body, the oxygen, the camps and the climbers to draw, and reads them each frame; what it owns is the picture's own: the camera's place and zoom, the points of the last two steps it draws between, the flakes, the motes and the puffs of snow, the warnings' clocks, each figure's pose and breath, its own seconds and the frame count. It imports the core and the physics, never the input or the page, and nothing outside it imports `pixi.js`.
- **The camera is the input's slope view.** `Camera` shows its `Zoom`'s metres of slope from the top of the screen to the bottom, on a phone as on a PC, with the climber at its anchor, a third from the left and below the middle. It maps the screen to slope metres and back, so the page hands it to `PlayerInput` as its `SlopeView`: a click lands in slope metres, to the centimetre, and the rendering needn't import the input to do it. It follows the climber where the picture draws them, between the steps, by a share of the way each frame that depends on the seconds gone, so a phone at 30 frames a second lands where a PC at 60 does.
- **The zoom is four frozen levels** (`Zoom`): 10, 20, 50 and 100 m of slope on the screen, 20 to start with. A `view` request (`[-]`, `[+]`, Z, X, the wheel, the d-pad's ← →) is the page's, like a `panel`: `ZoomSink` hands it to `ZoomControl`, which moves a level, keeps it on the device (`ZoomChoice`) and shows it between the buttons. The camera glides there in a quarter of a second around its anchor (`ZoomGlide`), and a click lands where this frame drew the slope, during the glide too. However far out, `LeastSize` keeps a climber 24 px tall and a line 1.5 px wide; above that, all is true to scale. The ground runs on level past the section's ends in the picture alone, and the ice in front fades out between 20 and 50 m.
- **A layer is drawn once, in metres with y up, and placed by the camera each frame** (`ParallaxLayer`): scaled to the camera's pixels a metre and moved by its depth, 1 for the slope's own, less for what's behind it, more for what's in front, 0 for what stays on the screen (the sky, the snow). A skyline's line rises from the ground's height at its depth, so under the climber it stays as high above them however far the section climbs; a far layer's vertical depth is next to nothing, or the ground ahead would fill the sky. What moves each frame is redrawn only when it moved, and once it's in view: a rope that lies still costs nothing, and one swaying in the wind off the screen nothing either.
- **What moves is drawn between the steps** (`SteppedPoints`, [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → Motion): each frame the scene is told how many steps it took and how far the next has come (`BetweenSteps`), and draws the climbers, their carabiners and the ropes' chains that far between the last two, a step behind the mountain; a jump further than a step goes is drawn at once. A rope is a smooth curve through its chain (`RopeCurve`), run through the jumar where the climber's hand holds it and swaying on the gust where it hangs free of the snow; a ladder across a crevasse gives under the climbers on it (`LadderBow`).
- **The background is the Khumbu's, in layers** (`SkylineLayer`, `MountainFaces`): named massifs over each layer's ridge, Pumori, Lingtren and Khumbutse across the valley behind the sea of clouds (`CloudSea`), the West Shoulder and Nuptse's fluted wall with its rock bands and the plume off its summit nearer, the Icefall's seracs nearer still, and the ice in front. The shapes are drawn white, once, and tinted each frame; the sea, the plume, the stars and the glow live inside the layers, so the quality's layer counts hold.
- **The light is the mountain clock's, and the colours one palette's** (`DayLight`, `Palette`, `Atmosphere`): the hour gives the sun's height, and from it the daylight, the alpenglow and its side, the stars and the headlamps; the palette mixes the sky, the air, the faces and the snow from the night's and the day's colours, and is mixed again only when the light has changed enough to show. As on #125's scene, the snow is navy at night, blue as the glow lights the sky before the sun, and white in it, its shade, track and ice the day's shares of it; the glow is pink before the sun is up and orange as it rises, on the horizon, the crests, the snow's edge and a climber's rim. The fixed colours, the suits, the rope, the tents, are the palette's statics, so a strong colour means one thing. `SlopeScene` writes the phase and the weather into `#slope`'s data attributes.
- **The weather is the hazards'** (`WeatherLook`, `Gust`): each weather has a look, how much of the snow falls, how far the far layers fade into the haze, how thick the veil behind the slope is; and a wind, gusting over the picture's seconds, which the spindrift along the snow (`Drift`), the plume and the slant of the falling snow all follow. Low quality keeps some spindrift: it tells the wind.
- **Every climber in the expedition is drawn** where the shared simulation puts them, the player's in the brighter suit, and with a white mark over their head in a crowd, four or more climbers on the screen (`Crowd`, `PlayerMark`, #87): in co-op the room's state is the one picture, each player's camera on their own climber. `ClimberFigure` draws a skeleton (`ClimberPose`, as `ClimberReading` reads the climber off the physics and the body) in a climber's kit as #125's scene draws one (#168): a rig of parts cut once from `FigureShapes`' outlines and moved onto the bones each frame (`FigureRig`), so a stride costs no drawing. `SuitRig` is the down suit with its baffles, dark yoke and shins, the boots with their gaiters and crampons, the mitts, the pack with its lid, the oxygen's bottle and the ice axe strapped on, the harness and its cowstails to the jumar and the carabiner, the hood, the mask with its valve and hose, on the face while their oxygen flows and hanging on the chest while it doesn't (#87), the goggles with the sky in them and the headlamp, each part shaded from where the light comes from and the far limbs darker and as cold as the sky; behind it `RimRig`'s silhouette rims the figure in the hour's light on the sun's side. It moves as [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → The animation shows the body's state has it: the boots planted on the ground the physics has, the snow's hummocks too, the rest step, a tired climber's shorter, lower steps, the jumar in the hand on the rope, a bend to the rope as a cowstail is clipped, the boots on a ladder's rungs, a fall, a hang below the rope and the getting up. It breathes (`Breathing`) faster with altitude, tiredness and effort, a cloud out of the mask's valve on each breath out, blown downwind, and each boot that comes down on the snow kicks up a puff (`SnowPuffs`). By the hour the headlamp shines: a soft beam drawn once and turned each frame, along the slope under the climber, a halo round the lamp and a pool where the beam comes down to the snow, and the spindrift's grains glint in it (`Beam`, the beam in the layer's metres). A dead climber lies flat and dim.
- **The hazards are shown, never written** (`HazardSigns`, `HazardDrawing`, #24): ice towers with broken tops, a shaded face and cracks stand over a serac's stretch and loaded snow lies along an avalanche slope or a cornice, so the player sees where not to linger; a hidden crevasse shows nothing. While one goes, its ice or snow comes down over the stretch as the warning runs out, lies there a moment after, and the whole picture shakes. The scene reads the hazards' warnings and keeps only its own clock of each.
- **The camps are rows of tents** (`CampSigns`, #26) on each camp's stretch of the ground, drawn behind the climbers, their sides rippling on the gust, with the prayer flags over Base Camp streaming on it (`PrayerFlags`), so the player sees where they set off from and where the section ends. Reaching the stretch of the camp the section ends at is the arrival: `CampNotice` (`src/interface/`) names the camp, reads the altimeter and the clock as the HUD does (`Readings`), and says how long the section took against the hours the sources give it, from `content/camps.json` through `Texts`; [Continue] or Esc closes it, and the climb goes on behind it. The camp itself comes with #53.
- **The quality is three frozen levels** (`GraphicsQuality`): which optional layers, how many flakes and motes, whether the sky's gradient is dithered, and a cap on the sharpness in device pixels a CSS pixel. The device starts on High, a phone (a coarse pointer) on Medium, and `QualityChoice` keeps the player's press in `localStorage` until the saves take the settings (#60): a `KeptChoice`, as `ZoomChoice` is. The `[Graphics: …]` button is a `setting-button`, not an `action-button`: it's the page's, not a control the game hears.
- **The picture has no clock and no chance of the expedition's.** It's paced by the browser's frames (`Ticker`), and what it draws by chance, the ridges, the stars, the sastrugi, the spindrift and the snow, comes from seeded generators of its own, so the same seed gives the same mountain and the expedition's draws are untouched. It reads the mountain's clock for the hour, never for its own pace: the twinkle, the breath, the gusts and what sways on them run on the picture's seconds.
- **The page's loop never waits for the mountain** (`GameLoop`): each frame the game takes the steps it's owed, and then the scene is drawn, told how many it took and how far the next has come. Alone, `SoloGame` turns the real seconds gone into whole steps through `StepPacer`, at most `SoloGame.MAX_STEPS_A_FRAME`, a second's, so a tab back from the background goes on where it paused, sends the player's queued actions with the first of them, and saves the climb on the device every ten seconds of play and when the tab is hidden. In a room, `RoomClient` takes the steps the room sent, in the room's order, and sends the player's actions to the room ([room-server.md](room-server.md)). A page request, a `panel` or a `view`, is the page's: both are a `GameActionSink`, which queues the game's actions alone.
- **Without WebGL the page says so** and runs the expedition with no picture, so the controls still work and the stories of the input hold.
- **The stories read what the scene says of itself.** Playwright can't read a WebGL canvas, so the scene writes the quality, the layers, the flakes, the frames, the camera, the metres it shows from top to bottom, the climber, the phase of the day and the weather into `#slope`'s data attributes, `window.everest.pixelAt(x, y)` reads a pixel back through Pixi's extract, and `window.everest.climberAt(climber)` says where any climber of the expedition is, in slope metres. No screenshots are compared: software WebGL draws differently on each machine. The frame rate is on the page (`#fps`) for a check by hand against the targets; headless browsers draw in software, so no story measures it. There each frame of the canvas is read back on the page's main thread, and every step of a story waits for it: the stories draw on Low, but for the picture's own in `e2e/slope.spec.ts`, and the rooms', with two or three browsers at once, on a small screen (#154).
- **The pure classes are tested in Node** (`Camera`, `Crowd`, `Zoom`, `ZoomControl`, `LeastSize`, `GraphicsQuality`, `KeptChoice`, `FrameRate`, `Snowfall`, `Skyline`, `MountainFaces`, `CloudSea`, `Stars`, `Drift`, `SlopeDetails`, `DayLight`, `Palette`, `Gust`, `WeatherLook`, `Atmosphere`, `SteppedPoints`, `ClimberReading`, `ClimberPose`, `FigureShapes`, `Breathing`, `Beam`, `SnowPuffs`, `RopeCurve`, `LadderBow`, `HazardSigns`, `CampSigns`, `PrayerFlags`, `GameLoop`, `ActionQueue`, `SoloGame`, `RoomClient`, `RoomServer`, `RoomLink`, `PlayerToken`, `ReloadOnce`, `Section`, `SlopeFacts`, `Readings`, `Texts`); the layers and the scene, which need a canvas, by `e2e/slope.spec.ts`, the Icefall, its tents and the arrival by `e2e/icefall.spec.ts`, the climb alone and the rooms by `e2e/alone.spec.ts` and `e2e/room.spec.ts`, and a PC and a phone climbing together in one room by `e2e/coop.spec.ts`. A story that begins far up the section edits the climb saved on the device (`climbFrom` in `e2e/climb.ts`) rather than walk there for minutes. A room's story can't: the room keeps the mountain's clock in real time, so it walks from Base Camp, at the faster pace.

`Section` reads the section the page plays from `content/route/` (the Khumbu Icefall, `icefall.json`, #26; [slope-physics.md](slope-physics.md) → Numbers); the massifs' places and the drawing's sizes are the picture's own numbers, not the mountain's (#149). The game's scale, how fast the climb goes by, is the knowledge base's `time.compression`, and the route is drawn at `route.drawn-scale` ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Real time).
