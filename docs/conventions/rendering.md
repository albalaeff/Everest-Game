# The rendering

`src/rendering/` draws the slope with PixiJS ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture): the ground, the ropes, the ladders and every climber as the slope physics has them, behind and in front of them the parallax layers, and the snow. `src/page/` puts the game together on the page: the start's choice, alone or in a room, the expedition, its loop, the camera, the scene and the controls. Both follow [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules and Objects; these are their own rules. How the picture looks and how fast it's drawn follow the design guidelines too: [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) and [../guidelines/performance.md](../guidelines/performance.md).

- **The rendering reads the simulation, and holds no game state.** `SlopeScene` is given a `SceneWorld`, the physics, the hazards, the mountain's clock, the body, the oxygen, the camps, the route's parts and the climbers to draw, and reads them each frame; what it owns is the picture's own: the camera's place and zoom, the points of the last two steps it draws between, the flakes, the motes and the puffs of snow, the warnings' clocks, each figure's pose and breath, its own seconds and the frame count. It imports the core and the physics, never the input or the page, and nothing outside it imports `pixi.js`.
- **The camera is the input's slope view.** `Camera` shows its `Zoom`'s metres of slope from the top of the screen to the bottom, on a phone as on a PC, with the climber at its anchor, a third from the left and below the middle; walking down the slope, the view slides slowly until they stand two thirds across, so the way ahead shows down as up, and a step back to clip an anchor doesn't swing it. It maps the screen to slope metres and back, so the page hands it to `PlayerInput` as its `SlopeView`: a click lands in slope metres, to the centimetre, and the rendering needn't import the input to do it. It follows the climber where the picture draws them, between the steps, by a share of the way each frame that depends on the seconds gone, so a phone at 30 frames a second lands where a PC at 60 does.
- **The zoom is four frozen levels** (`Zoom`): 10, 20, 50 and 100 m of slope on the screen, 20 to start with. A `view` request (`[-]`, `[+]`, Z, X, the wheel, the d-pad's ← →) is the page's, like a `panel`: `ZoomSink` hands it to `ZoomControl`, which moves a level, keeps it on the device (`ZoomChoice`) and shows it between the buttons. The camera glides there in a quarter of a second around its anchor (`ZoomGlide`), and a click lands where this frame drew the slope, during the glide too. However far out, `LeastSize` keeps a climber 24 px tall and a line 1.5 px wide; above that, all is true to scale. The ground runs on level past the section's ends in the picture alone, and the ice in front fades out between 20 and 50 m.
- **A layer is drawn once, in metres with y up, and placed by the camera each frame** (`ParallaxLayer`): scaled to the camera's pixels a metre and moved by its depth, 1 for the slope's own, less for what's behind it, more for what's in front, 0 for what stays on the screen (the sky, the snow). A layer behind the slope is seen in perspective (`Perspective`): it lies as far behind the slope as its depth says at the standard view, so zoomed out to 100 m the slope shrinks to a fifth and the peaks only to about half, their summits still in the sky. A skyline's line rises from the ground's height at its depth, so under the climber it stays as high above them however far the section climbs; a far layer's vertical depth is next to nothing, or the ground ahead would fill the sky. What moves each frame is redrawn only when it moved, and once it's in view: a rope that lies still costs nothing, and one swaying in the wind off the screen nothing either.
- **What moves is drawn between the steps** (`SteppedPoints`, [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → Motion): each frame the scene is told how many steps it took and how far the next has come (`BetweenSteps`), and draws the climbers, their carabiners and the ropes' chains that far between the last two, a step behind the mountain; a jump further than a step goes is drawn at once. A rope is a smooth curve through its chain (`RopeCurve`), run through the jumar where the climber's hand holds it and swaying on the gust where it hangs free of the snow; a ladder across a crevasse gives under the climbers on it (`LadderBow`).
- **A rope is drawn loaded where it's loaded** (#232, `gear.fixed-rope-under-weight`). The physics holds a climber by the rope's length and never stretches it ([slope-physics.md](slope-physics.md) → The rope); the picture shows the weight on it. Each cowstail's hold tells the rope how much of the climber's weight it bears there (`Held.load`, which `ClimberFigure` reads off the physics each frame): all of it hanging after a slip, lowering down a step or hauled out, on the jumar if it's on and on the carabiner if not; the arm's pull on the jumar between its strokes going up steep ground, harder the steeper (`Pose.pull`), and none on the flat. As far as it's loaded, the rope runs taut from the hold to each anchor above it, straight but where the snow or a lip stands in its way, which it bends over as a string pulled tight does (`RopeCurve.tautWay`); it turns a corner at the hold, taut above and slack below, and doesn't flutter there. A rope pulled tight by hand (`Held.pulled`: the haul held, as the Sherpa holds a ladder's line or hauls a client out) runs taut both ways, more of a rail. A hold's taut way stops at the next hold on the rope that bears weight (#239): hauling, the rope runs through the hauler's two hands (the figure draws its hold between them, so it moves with his pull), straight over the lip to the carabiner of whoever hangs on it, and from his hands back to its anchor, not on across the crevasse. The one who hangs is posed hanging for as long as the rope holds them off the ground (`ClimberView.hanging`), upright from the harness and leaning the rope's way, wherever their carabiner is drawn; with it level with them, hauled toward a lip, they used to be posed as falling and tumbled in the air. Whoever holds the haul faces along the rope in their hands, toward its far end (`ClimberReading`), whichever way they last walked: the crevasse its line crosses, and whoever hangs on it or crosses by it. Holding a ladder's line with nobody to haul, the Sherpa braces as he does to haul, eased, both hands still on the line (`PoseState.bracing`), where he used to stand with his back to the ladder. A climber whose weight comes onto the rope sinks by its stretch, the knowledge base's `gear.fixed-rope-stretch` of the rope between them and the anchor that holds them, bobs on it once or twice, slower on a longer rope, and hangs still, the rope quivering across itself as it comes taut (`RopeGive`); the picture alone draws them that much lower, 0.3 m at the most.
- **The background is the Khumbu's, in layers** (`SkylineLayer`, `MountainFaces`): named massifs over each layer's ridge, from Cholatse to Chomo Lonzo across the valley over the sea of clouds in rows (`CloudSea`); nearer, Pumori's horn behind Base Camp, the West Shoulder hiding Everest's summit, Nuptse's fluted wall and Lhotse's face ahead, with their rock bands, the plumes off their summits (`Plume`) and the fog banks drifting across them; the Icefall's seracs nearer still, a line of ice towers with snow on their tops (`IceTowers`), as big as the part of the route they stand by, and none behind the Western Cwm, which opens out behind Camp 1; and the ice in front, a block here and there, which keeps to the bottom of the screen as the climber goes up and down, never rising over the route ahead (#184). A massif's flank away from the sun is in its shade (`MountainFaces.shades`): the left ones in the morning, the right ones in the evening. The shapes are drawn white, once, and tinted each frame; the sea, the plumes, the fog, the stars and the glow live inside the layers, so the quality's layer counts hold, and the peaks are in every level's.
- **The light is the mountain clock's, and the colours one palette's** (`DayLight`, `Palette`, `Atmosphere`): the hour gives the sun's height, and from it the daylight, the alpenglow and its side, the stars and the headlamps; the palette mixes the sky, the air, the faces and the snow from the night's and the day's colours, the top of the sky deeper towards indigo the thinner the air over the player (`ThinAir`), and greyer the more of it is cloud; it's mixed again only when the light or the air has changed enough to show. As on #125's scene, the snow is navy at night, blue as the glow lights the sky before the sun, and white in it, its shade, track and ice the day's shares of it; the glow is pink before the sun is up and orange as it rises, on the horizon, the crests, the snow's edge and a climber's rim. The fixed colours, the suits, the rope, the tents, are the palette's statics, so a strong colour means one thing. `SlopeScene` writes the phase and the weather into `#slope`'s data attributes.
- **The weather is the hazards'** (`WeatherLook`, `Gust`): each weather has a look, how much of the snow falls, how far the far layers fade into the haze, how thick the veil behind the slope is, how much of the sky is cloud, how thick the fog banks across the peaks are and the mist in front of the slope, which clears around the climber; and a wind, gusting over the picture's seconds, which the blown snow, the plumes and the slant of the falling snow all follow. The blown snow (`BlownSnow`, #223) is soft, see-through puffs in the snow's colour for the hour, brighter in a headlamp's beam, none in a calm: in a breeze thin veils skim the surface ankle-high, along its rises and dips, and a wisp trails downwind off each edge, a lip, a wall's top, a serac's (`BlownSnow.edgesOf`, the towers' tops); in a strong wind the veils ride knee-high and the wisps stream into plumes a few metres long. A veil rises over what it can follow, dies against a wall, and over a lip goes on and sinks into the crevasse. A gust on its way (#197, `Atmosphere.blowing`) sends a wave of snow from upwind across the view in a second or two, waist-high, the climber half hidden in it, which settles over a few seconds once the gust has gone. Each puff shows more where fewer share the view, so Low keeps the veils, which tell the wind; the air's ice crystals still glint in the beams, faintly with no wind (`Drift`).
- **Every climber in the expedition is drawn** where the shared simulation puts them, the player's in the brighter suit, their Sherpa's in blue (`Palette.SHERPA_SUIT`) with a pack bigger than a client's (`SuitRig.LOADED`, #212), and with a white mark over their head in a crowd, four or more climbers on the screen (`Crowd`, `PlayerMark`, #87): in co-op the room's state is the one picture, each player's camera on their own climber. `ClimberFigure` draws a skeleton (`ClimberPose`, as `ClimberReading` reads the climber off the physics and the body) in a climber's kit as #125's scene draws one (#168): a rig of parts cut once from `FigureShapes`' outlines and moved onto the bones each frame (`FigureRig`), so a stride costs no drawing. `SuitRig` is the down suit with its baffles, dark yoke and shins, the boots with their crampons and their gaiters, on the shins and turning in the boots' shells as the ankles bend (#175), the mitts, the pack with its lid and the oxygen's bottle, the ice tools, a mountaineer's axe and a second, technical tool if the pack holds one, each with a dark edge so it reads on the snow and the night's ice as the rope does, in a hand or strapped head down to the pack (`FigureShapes`, `Palette.AXE_SHAFT`), the harness and its cowstails to the jumar and the carabiner, the hood, the mask with its valve and hose, on the face while their oxygen flows and hanging on the chest while it doesn't (#87), the goggles with the sky in them and the headlamp, each part shaded from where the light comes from and the far limbs darker and as cold as the sky; behind it `RimRig`'s silhouette rims the figure in the hour's light on the sun's side. On steps likely to slip (#208), a climber wobbles, their body swaying and their arms out for balance, more the likelier: a hurried step on blue ice the most, a careful one hardly (`ClimberReading` reads it off `ClimberView.slipChance`). There's no bar over the head: the player reads the ice's shine, the ladder's bounce and the wobble, as a climber would. It moves as [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → The animation shows the body's state has it: the boots planted on the ground the physics has, the snow's hummocks too, and stiff, turning onto the toe or the heel where a shin would lean further in its boot than the boot lets it (#175), strides that grow with the speed so the steps come no faster than a few a second, the mountain's clock outrunning the drawn route (`ClimberPose.STEPS_A_SECOND`, #222), the leg coming down taking the weight over the end of its swing so the hips settle rather than drop, careful steps (C) kept low, the arms stiller and the eyes on the feet, pausing on the locked back leg where they go slowly, up steep ground, each step deciding at its start so the change shows no jolt, a tired climber's shorter, lower steps, the jumar in the hand on the rope and the second carabiner on its short sling, the rope running through both, lifted off the snow to them (#202), a bend to the rope as a cowstail is clipped, the boots on a ladder's rungs, up a wall the head looking up it, across its top as the eyes come level with it and down onto it once over it (#222), a fall, a hang below the rope and the getting up. On a face of ice the boots kick in on their front points in short steps, the boots near level at a halt, each kick knocking a few chips of ice off, and the tools are swung as a climber places them (`IceTools`): each keeps to its hand, the axe in the near one and the second tool in the far one; a new pick in the ice is the free tool swung into it, levered out of the pick it hung from on the axe alone, the lower one on two, or for the first drawn off the pack over the shoulder; cocked behind the head, it strikes with a flick of the wrist, the head whipping through last, shudders as it bites, and the weight sinks onto it; chips of ice fly off where it went in, more and bigger plates where it bites poorly, glinting in the headlamp at night (`IceChips`). With one tool the far hand holds the jumar on the rope, or braces on the ice. Off the ice a tool comes out and goes back onto the pack after a moment, at once for a ladder's rungs, and in a fall it stays in the hand. It breathes (`Breathing`) faster with altitude, tiredness and effort, a cloud out of the mask's valve on each breath out, blown downwind, and each boot that comes down on the snow kicks up a puff (`SnowPuffs`). By the hour the headlamp shines: a soft beam drawn once and turned each frame, along the slope under the climber, or up a wall where the eyes look (`Pose.gaze`, #222), swinging round over a moment, a halo round the lamp and a pool where the beam comes down to the snow, the air's ice crystals glinting in it and the blown snow brighter in it (`Beam`, the beam in the layer's metres). The beam lights the ground as it is, not the boots' (`PoseState.terrain`): the cone is cut by the fan of its rays, each stopping where it meets the snow or a wall's face (`Beam.fan`), and the pool lies along what it lights, upright on a wall (#202). A dead climber lies flat and dim. A climber who turns to face the viewer (`ClimberView.towardViewer`, #235) is drawn from the front where they stand, nothing else in the picture changing: the side figure narrows to its edge and the front one opens out of it (`FacingTurn`, a quarter turn in about a fifth of a second; the turn right round, side to side, as before), `FrontRig` the same kit seen head on: the hood, the goggles with the sky in them, the mask, the suit's chest and arms, the harness with both cowstails, the legs and the boots apart, the pack's edges and the axe's head behind the shoulders. Facing out, the headlamp isn't a beam: the cone and its pool on the snow would point at the player, so the lamp shows as a small bright core with a soft glow and short rays round it, no wider than the hood (`ClimberFigure.GLOW`), and a faint pool of light at the boots; it never whites out the face or the screen, and the night's exposure is the scene's as before. The ropes aren't lifted to the hands while they face out.
- **The ground is the glacier's** (#172, #184): from Crampon Point, where the route's `moraine` part ends, a cap of snow marked with sastrugi and the boot track of the climbers before (`SlopeDetails`), but never on a wall's bare ice; under it blue ice in layers that darken as they go down, and run on under each crevasse, closed at its lips (`IceSurface`, `IceBody`); behind it the rubble Base Camp stands on, with stones on it (`Glacier`), navy at night as the snow is, but darker, and grey by day. Each crevasse a ladder crosses is a crack drawn a hand's breadth rougher than the ground's line (`Crevasse`): blue at its lips and black 9 m down, the light through the ice bright along its walls under their lips, snow plugging its foot and lying on its gentler steps. Each wall a ladder climbs, and each step of ice the route climbs with none (`SlopePhysics.bareFaces`), bares its blue ice, streaked down from its top (`IceWall`); a band of brittle ice across a step is pale and chalky, its edges ragged and cracked into the sound ice, so the player sees where a pick bites poorly (`IceWall.brittle`, `Palette.BRITTLE`); and a lip of snow stands out over every crevasse's edge and every wall's top, icicles hanging from it (`Cornice`). A climber on their tools, or lowering themselves down a step on its rope, is posed against its face as on a ladder up it, from its foot to its lip, so the pose knows where it ends (`ClimberReading`, #222). Blocks and towers of ice stand behind the route, in the air's haze so the route reads first, as big as its part has them: the Popcorn's jumble, the maze's towers, the Football Field's low ice, the Upper Icefall's seracs, the Cwm's open snow, nothing on the moraine (`IceTowers`). The route's blue ice (#190) is a glossy skin over the ground with a wet sheen along its top and glints, and no snow crest, so the player reads from afar where their footing will go. The snow around Camp 1 has small cracks; and a ladder longer than 3 m is lashed from sections, the cord wrapped round its rails at each joint; one across a crevasse bounces as the physics has it (`SlopePhysics.ladderBounce`), most in its middle, its rails, its hand line and the climbers on it bobbing together. All but the ladders are drawn once, in the day's colours or in white, and tinted by the hour's light.
- **The hazards are shown, never written** (`HazardSigns`, `HazardDrawing`, #24): seracs 15 to 21 m tall tower behind a serac's stretch, one every 10 m or so along a long one, so one stands over wherever a climber is (#229), leaning out over the route, their tops broken and snowed on, a face in shade and cracks down their ice, each standing on the ice about it, never down a crevasse (#184), and loaded snow lies along an avalanche slope or a cornice, so the player sees where not to linger; a hidden crevasse shows nothing. While an avalanche or a cornice goes, its snow comes down over the stretch as the warning runs out, lies there a moment after, and the whole picture shakes. A serac falls as one does (`SeracFall`, #229): a zone that goes breaks the serac over each climber in it. A crack runs across it a quarter to a little under half its height up, where the view shows it over the climbers' heads, chips falling off it and snow sloughing off its top as it leans out on the crack; its top breaks off, tips over its front edge and falls in blocks at the world's gravity, the snow on them, tumbling, the first landing on the step the game's comes down on: the picture's clock of a collapse is the game's steps, and it catches up with the strike. Each block shatters on the route across the stretch, its shards hopping and its chunks sliding to rest, in a cloud of ice dust that rises and grows and rolls away on the wind; the picture shakes a little as it cracks, more as it falls and hardest as each block lands (`SlopeLayer.shaking`, from nothing to about 1). Its stump, with the fresh blue ice of its break along its top, and its rubble stay for the rest of the climb; a stump still 4 m tall can break again, lower. Its draws are its own, from its stretch and the step it comes down on, and its ice is drawn apart from its air, so a reload lays the same stump and rubble again from the strikes; a zone's after a reload breaks a serac the draws pick, since the climbers' places aren't saved. The scene reads the hazards' warnings and strikes and keeps only its own picture of them.
- **The camps are rows of tents** (`CampSigns`, #26) on each camp's stretch of the ground, drawn behind the climbers, their sides rippling on the gust, with the prayer flags over Base Camp streaming on it (`PrayerFlags`), so the player sees where they set off from and where the section ends. Reaching the stretch of the camp the section ends at is the arrival: `CampNotice` (`src/interface/`) names the camp, reads the altimeter and the clock as the HUD does (`Readings`), and says how long the section took against the hours the sources give it, from `content/camps.json` through `Texts`; [Continue] or Esc closes it, and the climb goes on behind it. The player's death opens it too, once their body has come to rest, titled "You died", with [Start over] alone, which reloads the page past its start's choice to Your climber (#194). Both give the debrief (`Debrief`, `content/debrief.json`): a verdict, the cause of a death traced back to the mistake it came from, and the climb in lines, coloured by how each went, read off the logbook, the falls and the strikes, and a line for the gear check: the checks skipped, a fall on a loose crampon traced back to the one at Base Camp (#196). The HUD's sun clock (`SunClock`) says when the sun comes onto the ice, by the hazards' own hours, and that it's on it, in red; on a phone it's a line in the clock's box. The camp itself comes with #53.
- **The quality is three frozen levels** (`GraphicsQuality`): which optional layers, how many flakes and motes, whether the sky's gradient is dithered, and a cap on the sharpness in device pixels a CSS pixel. The device starts on High, a phone (a coarse pointer) on Medium, and `QualityChoice` keeps the player's press in `localStorage` until the saves take the settings (#60): a `KeptChoice`, as `ZoomChoice` is. The `[Graphics: …]` button is a `setting-button`, not an `action-button`: it's the page's, not a control the game hears.
- **The picture has no clock and no chance of the expedition's.** It's paced by the browser's frames (`Ticker`), and what it draws by chance, the ridges, the stars, the sastrugi, the blown snow and the falling snow, comes from seeded generators of its own, so the same seed gives the same mountain and the expedition's draws are untouched. It reads the mountain's clock for the hour, never for its own pace: the twinkle, the breath, the gusts and what sways on them run on the picture's seconds.
- **The page's loop never waits for the mountain** (`GameLoop`): each frame the game takes the steps it's owed, and then the scene is drawn, told how many it took and how far the next has come. Alone, `SoloGame` turns the real seconds gone into whole steps through `StepPacer`, at most `SoloGame.MAX_STEPS_A_FRAME`, a second's, so a tab back from the background goes on where it paused, sends the player's queued actions with the first of them, and saves the climb on the device every ten seconds of play and when the tab is hidden. In a room, `RoomClient` takes the steps the room sent, in the room's order, and sends the player's actions to the room ([room-server.md](room-server.md)). A page request, a `panel` or a `view`, is the page's: both are a `GameActionSink`, which queues the game's actions alone.
- **The training events are drawn** (#209): a staged serac collapse breaks the serac standing right over its stretch (`HazardSigns.seracs` moves the nearest there, or `seracOver` stands one over a stretch off the zones), as a zone's does (#229), its crack running as long as the training's, so the player sees it, stops and backs off; its rubble lies across the stretch. A ladder shakes as a real one does (`LadderShake`, #228): one staged to break creaks in jerks, its failing anchor slipping a few times at uneven moments, its end dropping a little further each time, the ladder ringing after each and trembling all the while; then it swings down from the end that holds into the crevasse, knocks against the wall and hangs there, swaying, until two climbers haul it back: it comes round again as they pull, and bounces once on the lip as it's laid across. A lashed ladder whose twist is on its way rocks over its long axis (#197), most at its middle and none at its anchored ends, its rails closing and opening as they turn, seen from a little above (`SlopeLayer.RAIL_DEPTH`), and settles once it's come; and a gust on its way blows the picture's wind up over a second and a half, streaming the snow, and lets it die after (`SceneWorld.jolts`). A climber hauling a partner leans back braced, sunk in the knees, both hands on the rope ahead pulling it in turn (`ClimberPose`), their legs still.
- **The gear check** (`GearCheck`, `src/page/`, #196) comes after Your climber, before every new climb alone: the player ticks what they check of their crampons, headlamp and water, each with why it matters, nothing ticked to begin with, on a screen that says it's a game model, not instructions; [Set off], or Enter from a box, sets them off with it (`Mountain.scenario`). A room's climbers set off with everything checked. A headlamp that has died throws no light (`Lamps.lit`).
- **The coach is the page's** (#192): `CoachCard` shows over the slope the tip `Coach` (`src/interface/`) picks each frame, reading the climb through `CoachedClimb`, the physics' and the hazards' views of the player and the route, and holding only what the screen needs, what's been learnt and what it shows; it never takes a click from the slope. The tip's arrow (#233) is the page's too: `CoachArrow` puts `#pointer` on the slope each frame, through the camera as Pemba's bubble is, at the place `CoachPointer` finds for the tip, with the control to use there; `CoachedClimb` hands it the face of ice the player is at, where their next pick bites well, as a careful climber's footwork has it (`Footwork.pickUp`), and the edge a hanging partner comes up at. It hides while its place is off the screen.
- **Pemba speaks in a bubble over his head** (#212): `SherpaBubble` (`#sherpa`) shows the line `SherpaVoice` (`src/interface/`) picks each frame, reading the climb through `SherpaClimb`, the Sherpa's, the physics', the clock's and the coach's views, with his name, for as long as it takes to read; at the screen's edge nearest him, pointing his way, while he's off it (`data-edge`), and at the top when there's no picture. A warning he says at once, the coach's warnings his cues too, though he never names a key; what he does as soon as the line up is read; a part of the route, a fact or a story in a quiet moment, every number from the knowledge base (`SherpaFacts`); a joke a minute apart at most, never twice. He thinks as a Sherpa (#218): Chomolungma and the goddess the puja asks for leave, the Icefall crossed fast and often, the family at home; and he knows the mountain: its camps, its years (1953, 1996, 2012, 2014, 2015), the Sherpas' records, the water a climber loses up here, every number again the knowledge base's (`sherpas.*`, `history.*`, `accidents.*`, `route.*`, `body.water-loss`). Each cue has a few lines that take turns; the climb's seed picks which comes first, which of a part's facts and stories he tells and the jokes' order, so two climbs don't sound alike. His words are `content/sherpa.json`'s, written against Wikipedia's Signs of AI writing. On the arrival the camp's notice opens with his line, and the debrief names him when it was he who pulled the player out.
- **Without WebGL the page says so** and runs the expedition with no picture, so the controls still work and the stories of the input hold.
- **The stories read what the scene says of itself.** Playwright can't read a WebGL canvas, so the scene writes the quality, the layers, the flakes, the frames, the camera, the metres it shows from top to bottom, the climber, whether they're drawn side-on or facing the viewer (`data-facing`, #235), the phase of the day and the weather into `#slope`'s data attributes, `window.everest.pixelAt(x, y)` reads a pixel back through Pixi's extract, and `window.everest.climberAt(climber)` says where any climber of the expedition is, in slope metres. No screenshots are compared: software WebGL draws differently on each machine. The frame rate is on the page (`#fps`) for a check by hand against the targets; headless browsers draw in software, so no story measures it. There each frame of the canvas is read back on the page's main thread, and every step of a story waits for it: the stories draw on Low, but for the picture's own in `e2e/slope.spec.ts`, and the rooms', with two or three browsers at once, on a small screen (#154).
- **The pure classes are tested in Node** (`Camera`, `Crowd`, `Zoom`, `ZoomControl`, `LeastSize`, `GraphicsQuality`, `KeptChoice`, `FrameRate`, `Snowfall`, `Skyline`, `MountainFaces`, `CloudSea`, `Perspective`, `ThinAir`, `Plume`, `Stars`, `Drift`, `SlopeDetails`, `Glacier`, `IceSurface`, `IceBody`, `Crevasse`, `Cornice`, `IceWall`, `IceTowers`, `Wander`, `DayLight`, `Palette`, `Gust`, `WeatherLook`, `Atmosphere`, `SteppedPoints`, `ClimberReading`, `ClimberPose`, `IceTools`, `FacingTurn`, `FigureShapes`, `Breathing`, `Beam`, `SnowPuffs`, `IceChips`, `RopeCurve`, `RopeGive`, `LadderBow`, `LadderShake`, `HazardSigns`, `SeracFall`, `CampSigns`, `PrayerFlags`, `GameLoop`, `ActionQueue`, `SoloGame`, `RoomClient`, `RoomServer`, `RoomLink`, `PlayerToken`, `ReloadOnce`, `Section`, `SlopeFacts`, `Readings`, `Texts`); the layers and the scene, which need a canvas, by `e2e/slope.spec.ts`, the Icefall, its tents and the arrival by `e2e/icefall.spec.ts`, the climb alone and the rooms by `e2e/alone.spec.ts` and `e2e/room.spec.ts`, and a PC and a phone climbing together in one room by `e2e/coop.spec.ts`. A story that begins far up the section edits the climb saved on the device (`climbFrom` in `e2e/climb.ts`) rather than walk there for minutes. A room's story can't: the room keeps the mountain's clock in real time, so it walks from Base Camp, at the faster pace.

`Section` reads the section the page plays from `content/route/` (the Khumbu Icefall, `icefall.json`, #26; [slope-physics.md](slope-physics.md) → Numbers); the massifs' places and the drawing's sizes are the picture's own numbers, not the mountain's (#149). The game's scale, how fast the climb goes by, is the knowledge base's `time.compression`, and the route is drawn at `route.drawn-scale` ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Real time).
