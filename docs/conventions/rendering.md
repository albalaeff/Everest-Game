# The rendering

`src/rendering/` draws the slope with PixiJS ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture): the ground, the ropes, the ladders and every climber as the slope physics has them, behind and in front of them the parallax layers, and the snow. `src/page/` puts the game together on the page: the start's choice, alone or in a room, the expedition, its loop, the camera, the scene and the controls. Both follow [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules and Objects; these are their own rules.

- **The rendering reads the simulation, and holds no game state.** `SlopeScene` is given the physics and the climbers to draw, and reads them each frame; what it owns is the picture's own: the camera's place, the flakes, the frame count. It imports the core and the physics, never the input or the page, and nothing outside it imports `pixi.js`.
- **The camera is the input's slope view.** `Camera` shows `Camera.METRES_HIGH` metres of slope from the top of the screen to the bottom, on a phone as on a PC, with the climber at its anchor, a third from the left and below the middle. It maps the screen to slope metres and back, so the page hands it to `PlayerInput` as its `SlopeView`: a click lands in slope metres, to the centimetre, and the rendering needn't import the input to do it. It follows the climber by a share of the way each frame that depends on the seconds gone, so a phone at 30 frames a second lands where a PC at 60 does.
- **A layer is drawn once, in metres with y up, and placed by the camera each frame** (`ParallaxLayer`): scaled to the camera's pixels a metre and moved by its depth, 1 for the slope's own, less for what's behind it, more for what's in front, 0 for what stays on the screen (the sky, the snow). A skyline's line rises from the ground's height at its depth, so under the climber it stays as high above them however far the section climbs; a far layer's vertical depth is next to nothing, or the ground ahead would fill the sky. What moves each frame is redrawn only when it moved: a rope that lies still costs nothing.
- **Every climber in the expedition is drawn** where the shared simulation puts them, the player's in the brighter suit: in co-op the room's state is the one picture, each player's camera on their own climber.
- **The quality is three frozen levels** (`GraphicsQuality`): which optional layers, how many flakes, and a cap on the sharpness in device pixels a CSS pixel. The device starts on High, a phone (a coarse pointer) on Medium, and `QualityChoice` keeps the player's press in `localStorage` until the saves take the settings (#60). The `[Graphics: …]` button is a `setting-button`, not an `action-button`: it's the page's, not a control the game hears.
- **The picture has no clock and no chance of the expedition's.** It's paced by the browser's frames (`Ticker`), and what it draws by chance, the ridges and the snow, comes from seeded generators of its own, so the same seed gives the same mountain and the expedition's draws are untouched.
- **The page's loop never waits for the mountain** (`GameLoop`): each frame the game takes the steps it's owed, and then the scene is drawn. Alone, `SoloGame` turns the real seconds gone into whole steps through `StepPacer`, at most `SoloGame.MAX_STEPS_A_FRAME`, sends the player's queued actions with the first of them, and saves the climb on the device every ten seconds of play and when the tab is hidden. In a room, `RoomClient` takes the steps the room sent, in the room's order, and sends the player's actions to the room ([room-server.md](room-server.md)). A `panel` request is the page's and isn't sent.
- **Without WebGL the page says so** and runs the expedition with no picture, so the controls still work and the stories of the input hold.
- **The stories read what the scene says of itself.** Playwright can't read a WebGL canvas, so the scene writes the quality, the layers, the flakes, the frames, the camera and the climber into `#slope`'s data attributes, `window.everest.pixelAt(x, y)` reads a pixel back through Pixi's extract, and `window.everest.climberAt(climber)` says where any climber of the expedition is, in slope metres. No screenshots are compared: software WebGL draws differently on each machine. The frame rate is on the page (`#fps`) for a check by hand against the targets; headless browsers draw in software, so no story measures it.
- **The pure classes are tested in Node** (`Camera`, `GraphicsQuality`, `FrameRate`, `Snowfall`, `Skyline`, `GameLoop`, `ActionQueue`, `SoloGame`, `RoomClient`, `RoomServer`, `RoomLink`, `PlayerToken`, `ReloadOnce`, `StandInRoute`); the layers and the scene, which need a canvas, by `e2e/slope.spec.ts`, and the climb alone and the rooms by `e2e/alone.spec.ts` and `e2e/room.spec.ts`.

`StandInRoute` and `content/route/stand-in.json`, a made-up section shaped like the Icefall, stand in until the real route comes (#26); the figures and the ridges' shapes, until the final style (#86). The game's scale, how fast the climb goes by, is the knowledge base's `time.compression` ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Real time).
