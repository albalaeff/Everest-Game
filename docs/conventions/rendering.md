# The rendering

`src/rendering/` draws the slope with PixiJS ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture): the ground, the ropes, the ladders and every climber as the slope physics has them, behind and in front of them the parallax layers, and the snow. `src/page/` puts the game together on the page: the start's choice, alone or in a room, the expedition, its loop, the camera, the scene and the controls. Both follow [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules and Objects; these are their own rules. How the picture looks and how fast it's drawn follow the design guidelines too: [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) and [../guidelines/performance.md](../guidelines/performance.md).

- **The rendering reads the simulation, and holds no game state.** `SlopeScene` is given a `SceneWorld`, the physics, the hazards, the mountain's clock, the body, the oxygen, the camps, the route's parts and the climbers to draw, and reads them each frame; what it owns is the picture's own: the camera's place and zoom, the points of the last two steps it draws between, the flakes, the motes and the puffs of snow, the warnings' clocks, each figure's pose and breath, its own seconds and the frame count. It imports the core and the physics, never the input or the page, and nothing outside it imports `pixi.js`.
- **The camera is the input's slope view.** `Camera` shows its `Zoom`'s metres of slope from the top of the screen to the bottom, on a phone as on a PC, with the climber at its anchor, a third from the left and below the middle; walking down the slope, the view slides slowly until they stand two thirds across, so the way ahead shows down as up, and a step back to clip an anchor doesn't swing it. It maps the screen to slope metres and back, so the page hands it to `PlayerInput` as its `SlopeView`: a click lands in slope metres, to the centimetre, and the rendering needn't import the input to do it. It follows the climber where the picture draws them, between the steps, by a share of the way each frame that depends on the seconds gone, so a phone at 30 frames a second lands where a PC at 60 does; a climber two screens away or more, put there at once by the god mode (#245), it cuts to rather than sweep across the route (`Camera.CUT_SCREENS`).
- **The zoom is four frozen levels** (`Zoom`): 10, 20, 50 and 100 m of slope on the screen, 20 to start with. A `view` request (`[-]`, `[+]`, Z, X, the wheel, the d-pad's ← →) is the page's, like a `panel`: `ZoomSink` hands it to `ZoomControl`, which moves a level, keeps it on the device (`ZoomChoice`) and shows it between the buttons. The camera glides there in a quarter of a second around its anchor (`ZoomGlide`), and a click lands where this frame drew the slope, during the glide too. However far out, `LeastSize` keeps a climber 24 px tall and a line 1.5 px wide; above that, all is true to scale. The ground runs on level past the section's ends in the picture alone, and the ice in front fades out between 20 and 50 m.
- **A layer is drawn once, in metres with y up, and placed by the camera each frame** (`ParallaxLayer`): scaled to the camera's pixels a metre and moved by its depth, 1 for the slope's own, less for what's behind it, more for what's in front, 0 for what stays on the screen (the sky, the snow). A layer behind the slope is seen in perspective (`Perspective`): it lies as far behind the slope as its depth says at the standard view, so zoomed out to 100 m the slope shrinks to a fifth and the peaks only to about half, their summits still in the sky. A skyline's line rises from the ground's height at its depth, so under the climber it stays as high above them however far the section climbs; a far layer's vertical depth is next to nothing, or the ground ahead would fill the sky. What moves each frame is redrawn only when it moved, and once it's in view: a rope that lies still costs nothing, and one swaying in the wind off the screen nothing either.
- **What moves is drawn between the steps** (`SteppedPoints`, [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → Motion): each frame the scene is told how many steps it took and how far the next has come (`BetweenSteps`), and draws the climbers, their carabiners and the ropes' chains that far between the last two, a step behind the mountain; a jump further than a step goes is drawn at once. A rope is a smooth curve through its chain (`RopeCurve`), run through the jumar where the climber's hand holds it and swaying on the gust where it hangs free of the snow; it runs through what holds it in the order they're drawn along it, so on a ladder, where the physics has the jumar and the second carabiner on one spot, it goes from the harness up to the hand and never doubles back between them (#250); a ladder across a crevasse gives under the climbers on it (`LadderBow`).
- **A rope is drawn loaded where it's loaded** (#232, `gear.fixed-rope-under-weight`). The physics holds a climber by the rope's length and never stretches it ([slope-physics.md](slope-physics.md) → The rope); the picture shows the weight on it. Each cowstail's hold tells the rope how much of the climber's weight it bears there (`Held.load`, which `ClimberFigure` reads off the physics each frame): all of it hanging after a slip, lowering down a step or hauled out, on the jumar if it's on and on the carabiner if not; the arm's pull on the jumar between its strokes going up steep ground, harder the steeper (`Pose.pull`), and none on the flat, nor on a ladder, whose rungs bear the weight (#250). As far as it's loaded, the rope runs taut from the hold to each anchor above it, straight but where the snow or a lip stands in its way, which it bends over as a string pulled tight does (`RopeCurve.tautWay`); it turns a corner at the hold, taut above and slack below, and doesn't flutter there; on its way it runs through every hand and carabiner on the rope, turning a corner at each rather than straight past them (#250). A rope pulled tight by hand (`Held.pulled`: the haul held, as the Sherpa holds a ladder's line or hauls a client out) runs taut both ways, more of a rail. A hold's taut way stops at the next hold on the rope that bears weight (#239): hauling, the rope runs through the hauler's two hands (the figure draws its hold between them, so it moves with his pull), straight over the lip to the carabiner of whoever hangs on it, and from his hands back to its anchor, not on across the crevasse. The one who hangs is posed hanging for as long as the rope holds them off the ground (`ClimberView.hanging`), upright from the harness and leaning the rope's way, wherever their carabiner is drawn; with it level with them, hauled toward a lip, they used to be posed as falling and tumbled in the air. Whoever holds the haul faces along the rope in their hands, toward its far end (`ClimberReading`), whichever way they last walked: the crevasse its line crosses, and whoever hangs on it or crosses by it. Holding a ladder's line with nobody to haul, the Sherpa braces as he does to haul, eased, both hands still on the line (`PoseState.bracing`), where he used to stand with his back to the ladder. A climber whose weight comes onto the rope sinks by its stretch, the knowledge base's `gear.fixed-rope-stretch` of the rope between them and the anchor that holds them, bobs on it once or twice, slower on a longer rope, and hangs still, the rope quivering across itself as it comes taut (`RopeGive`); the picture alone draws them that much lower, 0.3 m at the most.
- **The background is the Khumbu's, in layers** (`SkylineLayer`, `MountainFaces`): named massifs over each layer's ridge, from Cholatse to Chomo Lonzo across the valley over the sea of clouds in rows (`CloudSea`); nearer, Pumori's horn behind Base Camp, the West Shoulder hiding Everest's summit, Nuptse's fluted wall and Lhotse's face ahead, with their rock bands, the plumes off their summits (`Plume`) and the fog banks drifting across them; the Icefall's seracs nearer still, a line of ice towers with snow on their tops (`IceTowers`), as big as the part of the route they stand by, and none behind the Western Cwm, which opens out behind Camp 1; and the ice in front, a block here and there, which keeps to the bottom of the screen as the climber goes up and down, never rising over the route ahead (#184). A massif's flank away from the sun is in its shade (`MountainFaces.shades`): the left ones in the morning, the right ones in the evening. The shapes are drawn white, once, and tinted each frame; the sea, the plumes, the fog, the stars and the glow live inside the layers, so the quality's layer counts hold, and the peaks are in every level's.
- **The light is the mountain clock's, and the colours one palette's** (`DayLight`, `Palette`, `Atmosphere`): the hour gives the sun's height, and from it the daylight, the alpenglow and its side, the stars and the headlamps; the palette mixes the sky, the air, the faces and the snow from the night's and the day's colours, the top of the sky deeper towards indigo the thinner the air over the player (`ThinAir`), and greyer the more of it is cloud; it's mixed again only when the light or the air has changed enough to show. As on #125's scene, the snow is navy at night, blue as the glow lights the sky before the sun, and white in it, its shade, track and ice the day's shares of it; the glow is pink before the sun is up and orange as it rises, on the horizon, the crests, the snow's edge and a climber's rim. The fixed colours, the suits, the rope, the tents, are the palette's statics, so a strong colour means one thing. `SlopeScene` writes the phase and the weather into `#slope`'s data attributes.
- **The weather is the hazards'** (`WeatherLook`, `Gust`): each weather has a look, how much of the snow falls, how far the far layers fade into the haze, how thick the veil behind the slope is, how much of the sky is cloud, how thick the fog banks across the peaks are and the mist in front of the slope, which clears around the climber; and a wind, gusting over the picture's seconds, which the blown snow, the plumes and the slant of the falling snow all follow. The blown snow (`BlownSnow`, #223) is soft, see-through puffs in the snow's colour for the hour, brighter in a headlamp's beam, none in a calm: in a breeze thin veils skim the surface ankle-high, along its rises and dips, and a wisp trails downwind off each edge, a lip, a wall's top, a serac's (`BlownSnow.edgesOf`, the towers' tops); in a strong wind the veils ride knee-high and the wisps stream into plumes a few metres long. A veil rises over what it can follow, dies against a wall, and over a lip goes on and sinks into the crevasse. A gust on its way (#197, `Atmosphere.blowing`) sends a wave of snow from upwind across the view in a second or two, waist-high, the climber half hidden in it, which settles over a few seconds once the gust has gone. Each puff shows more where fewer share the view, so Low keeps the veils, which tell the wind; the air's ice crystals still glint in the beams, faintly with no wind (`Drift`).
- **Every climber in the expedition is drawn** where the shared simulation puts them, the player's in the brighter suit, their Sherpa's in blue (`Palette.SHERPA_SUIT`) with a pack bigger than a client's (`SuitRig.LOADED`, #212), and with a white mark over their head in a crowd, four or more climbers on the screen (`Crowd`, `PlayerMark`, #87): in co-op the room's state is the one picture, each player's camera on their own climber. `ClimberFigure` draws a skeleton (`ClimberPose`, as `ClimberReading` reads the climber off the physics and the body) in a climber's kit as #125's scene draws one (#168): a rig of parts cut once from `FigureShapes`' outlines and moved onto the bones each frame (`FigureRig`), so a stride costs no drawing. `SuitRig` is the down suit with its baffles, dark yoke and shins, the boots with their crampons and their gaiters, on the shins and turning in the boots' shells as the ankles bend (#175), the mitts, the pack with its lid and the oxygen's bottle, the ice tools, a mountaineer's axe and a second, technical tool if the pack holds one, each with a dark edge so it reads on the snow and the night's ice as the rope does, in a hand or strapped head down to the pack (`FigureShapes`, `Palette.AXE_SHAFT`), the harness and its cowstails to the jumar and the carabiner, the hood, the mask with its valve and hose, on the face while their oxygen flows and hanging on the chest while it doesn't (#87), the goggles with the sky in them and the headlamp, each part shaded from where the light comes from and the far limbs darker and as cold as the sky; behind it `RimRig`'s silhouette rims the figure in the hour's light on the sun's side. On steps likely to slip (#208), a climber wobbles, their body swaying and their arms out for balance, more the likelier: a hurried step on blue ice the most, a careful one hardly (`ClimberReading` reads it off `ClimberView.slipChance`). There's no bar over the head: the player reads the ice's shine, the ladder's bounce and the wobble, as a climber would. It moves as [../guidelines/graphics.md](../guidelines/graphics.md) → The animation shows the body's state has it: the boots planted on the ground the physics has, the snow's hummocks too, and stiff, turning onto the toe or the heel where a shin would lean further in its boot than the boot lets it (#175), strides that grow with the speed so the steps come no faster than a few a second, the mountain's clock outrunning the drawn route (`ClimberPose.STEPS_A_SECOND`, #222), the leg coming down taking the weight over the end of its swing so the hips settle rather than drop, careful steps (C) kept low, the arms stiller and the eyes on the feet, pausing on the locked back leg where they go slowly, up steep ground, each step deciding at its start so the change shows no jolt, a tired climber's shorter, lower steps, the jumar in the hand on the rope and the second carabiner on its short sling, the rope running through both, lifted off the snow to them (#202), a bend to the rope as a cowstail is clipped, the boots on a ladder's rungs, up a wall the head looking up it, across its top as the eyes come level with it and down onto it once over it (#222), a fall, a hang below the rope and the getting up. On a face of ice the boots kick in on their front points in short steps, the boots near level at a halt, each kick knocking a few chips of ice off, and the tools are swung as a climber places them (`IceTools`): each keeps to its hand, the axe in the near one and the second tool in the far one; a new pick in the ice is the free tool swung into it, levered out of the pick it hung from on the axe alone, the lower one on two, or for the first drawn off the pack over the shoulder; cocked behind the head, it strikes with a flick of the wrist, the head whipping through last, shudders as it bites, and the weight sinks onto it; chips of ice fly off where it went in, more and bigger plates where it bites poorly, glinting in the headlamp at night (`IceChips`). With one tool the far hand holds the jumar on the rope, or braces on the ice. Off the ice a tool comes out and goes back onto the pack after a moment, at once for a ladder's rungs, and in a fall it stays in the hand. It breathes (`Breathing`) faster with altitude, tiredness and effort, a cloud out of the mask's valve on each breath out, blown downwind, and each boot that comes down on the snow kicks up a puff (`SnowPuffs`). By the hour the headlamp shines: a soft beam drawn once and turned each frame, along the slope under the climber, or up a wall where the eyes look (`Pose.gaze`, #222), swinging round over a moment, a halo round the lamp and a pool where the beam comes down to the snow, the air's ice crystals glinting in it and the blown snow brighter in it (`Beam`, the beam in the layer's metres). The beam lights the ground as it is, not the boots' (`PoseState.terrain`): the cone is cut by the fan of its rays, each stopping where it meets the snow or a wall's face (`Beam.fan`), and the pool lies along what it lights, upright on a wall (#202). A dead climber lies flat and dim. A climber who turns to face the viewer (`ClimberView.towardViewer`, #235) is drawn from the front where they stand, nothing else in the picture changing: the side figure narrows to its edge and the front one opens out of it (`FacingTurn`, a quarter turn in about a fifth of a second; the turn right round, side to side, as before), `FrontRig` the same kit seen head on, on the side figure's own bones (`FrontShapes`): the knees, the harness, the shoulders, the hands and the hood are as high as the side figure standing has them, each limb is as thick as its down (`FigureShapes.DOWN`) and quilted between the side's seams, so neither grows nor shrinks through the turn; it's painted as the side one is (`KitShading`), lit from the viewer's right, rimmed with the hour's light on the sun's side as the side one is. The down puffed between its seams, the dark yoke and the main zip up to the chin; the hood with its puffed rim round the face, the goggles with the sky in them and the headlamp on the brim; the mask on the face, or hanging on the chest with the face showing, and its hose over the left shoulder from the regulator on the bottle in the pack; the pack's lid over the shoulders, a Sherpa's higher; the straps down the chest and the chest strap; the harness with its leg loops, buckle, belay loop and a carabiner on each gear loop, both cowstails, and the jumar on the first; the mitts with their gauntlets; the dark shin panels, the gaiters with their collars and zips, and the boots with their rands and toe caps on the crampons' frames and points. The axe, strapped head down to the back of the pack, is hidden behind the body. Facing out, the headlamp isn't a beam: the cone and its pool on the snow would point at the player, so the lamp shows as a small bright core with a soft glow and short rays round it, no wider than the hood (`ClimberFigure.GLOW`), and a faint pool of light at the boots; it never whites out the face or the screen, and the night's exposure is the scene's as before. The ropes aren't lifted to the hands while they face out.
- **Off their feet, on a ladder's top and at one spot, a climber looks as one would** (#243). Held by the rope, they lean the way it pulls no further than a harness lets them (`ClimberPose.HANG_LEAN`: it ties in at the waist), with no jump as the rope comes up over them, and both cowstails run up to one point of the rope, never across each other. Beside a wall's face within a leg's reach (`ClimberReading` reads it off the ground, and turns them to it; one against a face is over its foot, not on its top) they brace their boots on it, the harness out from it, the legs near straight and the body leaning back, and walk them up it as the rope takes them up (`ClimberPose.bracedOn`): the last metre of a haul, and a slip on a face of ice. A ladder up a wall stands over the wall's top (`ClimberPose.LADDER_OVER`), as the Icefall's are set: the boots keep to its own rungs, never one over its top, and the hands hold its rails there to step off it or onto it. Climbers the physics has at one point of the route, a line, stand a step apart along the ground (`Abreast`): the player's own keeps their place and is drawn over the others, who step aside from those before them, never over a lip, and only on their feet on the ground, at a ladder's end on a lip too; the step is the picture's alone.
- **A casualty and their rescuer look as they would** (#241). A climber down with a bone broken sits where they came to rest (`ClimberPose`'s `sit`): a broken leg out straight, a hand on the thigh, the other propped behind; a broken arm held across the body; rocking a little with the pain, and cramped, knees up, in a crevasse's narrow foot. On a sprained leg they limp. What a landing left and what first aid has put on it shows on the near limb (`Dressing`): the blood of a bone broken through the skin, the tourniquet, the splint bound on with wrap, a broken arm's sling, a sprain's bandage. Their rescuer kneels at the hurt limb, bent to it, the hands working on it where it's drawn (`ClimberFigure.wound`), a step from where the physics has them (`Abreast`'s `beside`, past the limb's end), and is drawn over them; at a rescue line's anchor they kneel back from its screws, facing the lip (`LineAnchor`). The line is the slope layer's (`RescueLines`): two ice screws turned in one after the other and the sling that joins them as the anchor is built, then the yellow cord over the lip and down the ice, the fixed line keeping its orange. A climber on it holds it a reach above them (`LineHold`), going down and up as down a fixed rope; a casualty hauled up it hangs in the harness clear of the ice, the cord taut to them and no further, and back through a pulley to the hauler's hands. Carried, the casualty is drawn on the rescuer's back where the rescuer is drawn (`ClimberFigure.mount`), behind them, arms over their shoulders; the rescuer bends under them, hands under their knees, and both packs are off (`FigureRig.packOn`): a rescuer leaves theirs to carry a casualty. All of it is the picture's alone.
- **The ground is the glacier's** (#172, #184): from Crampon Point, where the route's `moraine` part ends, a cap of snow marked with sastrugi and the boot track of the climbers before (`SlopeDetails`), but never on a wall's bare ice; under it blue ice in layers that darken as they go down, and run on under each crevasse, closed at its lips (`IceSurface`, `IceBody`); behind it the rubble Base Camp stands on, with stones on it (`Glacier`), navy at night as the snow is, but darker, and grey by day. Each crevasse a ladder crosses is a crack drawn a hand's breadth rougher than the ground's line (`Crevasse`): blue at its lips and black 9 m down, the light through the ice bright along its walls under their lips, snow plugging its foot and lying on its gentler steps. Each wall a ladder climbs, and each step of ice the route climbs with none (`SlopePhysics.bareFaces`), bares its blue ice, streaked down from its top (`IceWall`); a band of brittle ice across a step is pale and chalky, its edges ragged and cracked into the sound ice, so the player sees where a pick bites poorly (`IceWall.brittle`, `Palette.BRITTLE`); and a lip of snow stands out over every crevasse's edge and every wall's top, icicles hanging from it (`Cornice`). A climber on their tools, or lowering themselves down a step on its rope, is posed against its face as on a ladder up it, from its foot to its lip, so the pose knows where it ends (`ClimberReading`, #222). Blocks and towers of ice stand behind the route, in the air's haze so the route reads first, as big as its part has them: the Popcorn's jumble, the maze's towers, the Football Field's low ice, the Upper Icefall's seracs, the Cwm's open snow, nothing on the moraine (`IceTowers`). The route's blue ice (#190) is a glossy skin over the ground with a wet sheen along its top and glints, and no snow crest, so the player reads from afar where their footing will go. The snow around Camp 1 has small cracks; and a ladder longer than 3 m is lashed from sections, the cord wrapped round its rails at each joint; one across a crevasse bounces as the physics has it (`SlopePhysics.ladderBounce`), most in its middle, its rails, its hand line and the climbers on it bobbing together. All but the ladders are drawn once, in the day's colours or in white, and tinted by the hour's light.
- **The hazards are shown, never written** (`HazardSigns`, `HazardDrawing`, #24): seracs 15 to 21 m tall tower behind a serac's stretch, one every 10 m or so along a long one, so one stands over wherever a climber is (#229), leaning out over the route, their tops broken and snowed on, a face in shade and cracks down their ice, each standing on the ice about it, never down a crevasse (#184), and loaded snow lies along an avalanche slope or a cornice as a drift, deepest along its middle and thinning to nothing at its ends (#243), so the player sees where not to linger; a hidden crevasse shows nothing. While an avalanche or a cornice goes, its snow comes down over the stretch as the warning runs out, lies there a moment after, and the whole picture shakes. A serac falls as one does (`SeracFall`, #229): a zone that goes breaks the serac over each climber in it. A crack runs across it a quarter to a little under half its height up, where the view shows it over the climbers' heads, chips falling off it and snow sloughing off its top as it leans out on the crack; its top breaks off, tips over its front edge and falls in blocks at the world's gravity, the snow on them, tumbling, the first landing on the step the game's comes down on: the picture's clock of a collapse is the game's steps, and it catches up with the strike. Each block shatters on the route across the stretch, its shards hopping and its chunks sliding to rest, in a cloud of ice dust that rises and grows and rolls away on the wind; the picture shakes a little as it cracks, more as it falls and hardest as each block lands (`SlopeLayer.shaking`, from nothing to about 1). Its stump, with the fresh blue ice of its break along its top, and its rubble stay for the rest of the climb; a stump still 4 m tall can break again, lower. Its draws are its own, from its stretch and the step it comes down on, and its ice is drawn apart from its air, so a reload lays the same stump and rubble again from the strikes; a zone's after a reload breaks a serac the draws pick, since the climbers' places aren't saved. The scene reads the hazards' warnings and strikes and keeps only its own picture of them.
- **The camps are tents to true size** (`CampSigns`, `TentDrawing`, #26, #258), where the section's file pitches them on each camp's stretch, so the player sees where they set off from and where the section ends. Each is an expedition dome as long and as high as the knowledge base has it (`TentFacts.sizes`): a gold fly over crossed poles, a grey vestibule in front with its zipped door and a smaller one behind, a vent, a valance on the ground, guy lines tied to rocks on the rubble and staked in the snow, and beside it a barrel, a duffel, oxygen bottles, a wand or an axe, never in a neighbour's doorway. Base Camp has its big dome besides, and both camps the other expeditions' tents, smaller and hazier, on the side away from the route. They sway on the gust, with the prayer flags over Base Camp streaming on it (`PrayerFlags`); at night their rooms glow through the fly, and one with someone in it shows their shadow.
- **A climber goes into a tent through its door** (#258). While the player stands at a door, [Enter the tent] is pinned over that tent (`TentDoorPin`, `#tent-door`, a `SlopePin`), with the tent's key on it where there's a keyboard; it sends the tent's action, as the key and the menu's [Tent] do. The slope then shows them through (`TentEntry`, in `ClimberFigure`): a step to the door, the flap open, down on hands and knees (`ClimberPose`'s crawl), in, and the flap falls; out the same way back. `#slope`'s `data-passage` says where they are: `outside`, `going-in`, `inside`, `coming-out`. A climber who's inside isn't drawn, and their Sherpa waits outside.
- **The tent's inside is a screen of the page's** (`TentScreen`, `src/page/tent/`, `#tent`, #258), open once the slope has shown the player in, and at once where there's no picture. It's SVG, as the menu is: the tent cut open from end to end, 300 px a metre from the same profile as the slope's (`TentArt`, `CampSigns.profile`), with the sky, the peaks, the neighbours' tents and the ground outside, rubble at Base Camp and snow above; the vestibule with the boots, the crampons and the stove on its board; the room with its mats, the sleeping bag, the pack with its tools, pockets, a loft, socks on a line, the lamp, and the guitar at Base Camp. `TentPicture` writes it into the stage, shows the bottles, meals, canisters, oxygen and gear there are, colours the outside by the hour and the weather (`Atmosphere`), darkens the tent at night and lights it by its lamp, out while they sleep, and frames it for the stage: the whole tent where it fits, and the part round what the person does where it doesn't, upright or on a phone on its side. The person is the slope's climber, sitting (#264): the bones of `ClimberPose` at 300 px a metre, the suit's shapes of `FrontShapes` (`DwellerShapes`), painted as `FrontRig` paints them, each part shaded across toward the lamp and each baffle of down puffed between its seams (`DwellerDrawing`); the hood down, the collar open on the base layer, down booties. The head is `DwellerHead`'s, in three dimensions at a real head's size and proportions: its cross-sections from the crown to the chin, turned a little to the stove and drawn as seen, and the face drawn front on from its measurements (`Features`: the cheeks, the jaw, the chin, the eyes, the brows, the nose, the lips, the ears, the stubble) and wrapped round it, so a character editor can set them; no outlines, but soft light and shade; eyes with lids that blink and irises that look about, lips over a jaw that opens, stubble with its grain, sunburn, the goggles' paler band and a ribbed beanie; the hands are `DwellerHands'` (#269), shaped for what they hold: open, wrapped round a bottle or a lump of snow, laid across a meal's pouch, closed tight on a lighter, the pot's handle or the spoon the pot is stirred with, or pinching a spoon, a bar or the guitar's pick as a pen is held. A hand is drawn in two layers with what it holds between them, in the thing's own frame: the palm behind a bottle and the fingers across its front, so the thing is in the hand, upright or tipped as the pose has it whichever way the wrist is bent; the pot, carried by the handle on its near side, is behind the whole arm. The bottle, the pouch, the pot and the spoon are the tent's own (`TentArt`): what a hand takes is hidden where it stood, and stands there again when it's put back. What goes into the pot or the pouch is cut off where it goes in: the spoon, the falling snow, the water. The upper arms are drawn behind the body, so each sleeve's round top runs on under the shoulder whichever way the arm turns, and an arm never comes away from the body; the forearms and the hands are in front of it. Each arm is three bones, the upper arm, the forearm and the hand (`DwellerPose`): the first two bent in three dimensions to the wrist and drawn flat, so an arm that reaches toward the viewer is drawn shorter, the elbow pointing the way the pose says and never behind the arm, where the forearm would be a sleeve seen end on; the hand turned at the wrist to what it holds, across it or along it, no further than a wrist bends, and what's left taken up by the thing in the fingers. **A pose asks for no more than that**: what's held points the way the pose aims it, so a bottle is upright as it leaves the floor and its mouth is at the lips, and the tests hold every movement to it, to a wrist's bend, and to no jump from one frame to the next. Every act has its pose (`DwellerActs` picks it from the tent's rule), its key poses eased from one to the next along curves: in and out on hands and knees, sitting, a drink from the bottle on the floor, a bar from the chest pocket, a hand in the pack, the guitar with a foot keeping time, lying down, asleep in the bag and waking with a stretch; a night the clock skipped plays as lying down, a moment asleep and waking. A chore at the stove is seen in its parts, each begun as the chore gets so far and each ending where the next begins: melting snow, the stove is lit, then the pot stirred with the spoon in the fist, the spoon left standing in it while a lump of snow comes from the sack and falls in; cooking, the stove is lit, the bottle's water poured into the pot, the hands held to the flame while it boils, a pouch taken from the pile and filled from the pot, sealed and left to stand, and eaten from with the spoon. When the act changes otherwise, a chore begun or cut short by a drink, the pose they were in turns into the new one's over a third of a second (`DwellerBlend`), and what's done aside from a chore leaves the stove burning. The flame, the steam, the notes, the breath of a sleeper and the falling snow are the style sheet's, and stop with reduced motion. A climber's look is a set of colours (`Appearance`), the picture's variables (`--suit`, `--yoke`, `--skin`, `--hair`, `--eyes`, `--hat`, `--booties`, `--base-layer`, each kit colour with its dark and lit tones), so customising it is another set.
- **The tent's panel is pictures and few words** (`TentReadout`, `content/tent.json`, #264): where the tent is and the time, and how the climber feels, a mark and the self-check's word for each; the water against the bottles, the gas, the meals, the bars and the oxygen, each drawn as the pack draws it (`#art-…`, with a canister and a meal's pouch of their own) with its number; and a button a chore, its mark in a ring and its word, with what it takes and gives as marks with numbers by the tents' numbers, the mark of what it lacks crossed out, or, in hand, the ring filling with its progress, the time left and its stop: pressed again, it stops. The marks are line drawings used as masks (`--icon-*`), never colour alone. Each button and row says the whole in a sentence, as its tooltip and its name to a screen reader. The stove's warning shows while it burns, its triangle red, as danger is, and the sleep slept stands by the clock until the next chore. Both give the debrief (`Debrief`, `content/debrief.json`): a verdict, the cause of a death traced back to the mistake it came from, and the climb in lines, coloured by how each went, read off the logbook, the falls and the strikes, and a line for the gear check: the checks skipped, a fall on a loose crampon traced back to the one at Base Camp (#196).
