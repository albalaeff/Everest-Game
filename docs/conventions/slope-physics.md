# The slope physics

`src/physics/` moves the climbers over the ground and holds them on fixed ropes ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture: "our own simple physics, rope using Verlet integration"). It's a rule of the core, `SlopePhysics`, run inside its step ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Rules), and it keeps the core's rules for the same result everywhere. It follows [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules, Objects, and Time and chance; these are its own rules.

## The slope

- **The ground** (`Terrain`) is a line of points in metres, from left to right: `x` across and `y` up. `x` never goes back, so a wall is two points at the same `x`, and an overhang can't be drawn. Past its ends the ground goes on flat.
- **The snow lies on a section's line** (`Snow`, #168), a ground of its own with its points `Snow.STEP` apart across: the line's hollows filled and its edges rounded, and over it the swells and hummocks of a glacier's snow, tens of centimetres high over a few metres, from a seeded generator of its own (`SnowLayout`). By every wall, along every ladder and over the layout's level stretches, the camps' and a few metres round them, where the climbs set off, it lies on the line, and it rises smoothly to its full relief past them. It's measured along the line under it, so whatever a layout lays by how far along the ground stays where it was, and it turns only at the line's corners, so a step stops there alone. A climber walks its slope as any other, so its hummocks slow them and tire them by what their gradients cost; on the Icefall they're never steeper than the slips' `technique.slip-free-steepness`. A save keeps the line and the snow's layout, which lays the same snow again.
- **A scenario lays out a section** (`SlopePhysics.start`): its ground and the snow on it, its ropes by where their anchors are along the ground, its ladders by where their ends are, and the climbers standing on it. The physics works them out in the layout's order.
- **A section is drawn at 1/`route.drawn-scale` of life**, 12, its length and its height. The mountain's clock runs faster than that, `time.compression`, 36, and the climber goes as many times faster than in life over the drawn ground, three times, so they take a section's real hours by the clock ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Real time, #167). The layout gives the real altitude of its two ends (`SlopeLayout.altitude`), and `SectionScale` reads the altimeter from it: the real rise by the share of the section's width crossed, and the drawn ground's own ups and downs at life size, so a 10 m fall reads 10 m. `climber(id).altitude` is that reading. Each section's test holds its drawn rise to the scale (`SectionScale.ratio`).
- **A climber is a point at their feet.** On their feet, they're somewhere `along` the ground or on a ladder; falling, they're a body under gravity. `climber(id)` is how the rendering and the rules above see them, with the rope each cowstail is clipped to, where its carabiner is on it (`firstAt`, `secondAt`, the first the jumar) and whether the jumar is held; `ropes` and `ladders` name what's laid, and `rope(id)` and `ladder(id)` say where a rope or a ladder lies.

## Walking and falls

- **Walking goes by what the steepness ahead costs** (`Walking`, #150). `walkingCost` is the energy a metre of walking takes of each kilogram moved, measured by gradient, the rise over the run, as the physiologists' treadmills give it; past the steepest gradient measured, each metre of height costs what it costs there, up to `steepest`. A climber goes as fast as their stride's share of `walkingPower` pays for, and no faster than `fastestWalk`: up a slope the power holds them back, down one and on the flat their footing does. Up a slope a stride pays the level's cost and `uphillCostShare` of the climb's extra over it, a playability tuning that eases the climb (#186): a slope never goes faster than the level, and the steeper it is the more it's eased. A step stops at a corner, so the next one takes the new ground's speed. Ground steeper up than `steepest` stops the climber; ground steeper down is a fall, unless they're clipped to the fixed rope that runs down it: then they stop at its edge, to lower themselves down it on the rope (below).
- **A climber faces a way, and can turn to face the viewer** (#235). `ClimberView.facing` is the way along the slope they last walked, up the route (1) from the start, kept when they stop; `towardViewer` says they've turned to look out of the slope at whoever watches. A `turn` action (`toward: "viewer"`) stops them and turns them, on their feet on the ground only: not in a fall, on a ladder, on the ice or lowering. The next walk either way turns them side-on again, and a fall does too. It changes nothing else: no speed, no slip, no exertion, so a replay and a room replay the same climb. A save keeps both, and one from before them faces up the route.
- **A step's exertion is the share of the stride's power it takes**: all of it where the power holds the climber back and on a ladder, less where the fastest walk does, and nought still. `climber(id).exertion` says what the last step took, which the body reads to tire them by it ([body.md](body.md) → Fatigue), and a save keeps it.
- **A slip is chance's, by the climber's care** (#208). Each metre a climber walks on steep snow, blue ice or a ladder has a chance of slipping: on snow `slipChance` by the steepness, none up to the slip-free steepness; on blue ice `footing.ice` more, and `footing.iceSteep` times that more for each unit of the slope's sine; on a ladder `ladderSlipChance` by its steepness, and `footing.bounce.slip` more for each unit of its bounce. It's divided by how sure-footed they are (`sureness`, the body's: the rest step's care makes them surer, the faster pace and tiredness less, [body.md](body.md)), and a step's chance is its metres': as likely a metre at any speed. The step draws from the expedition's seeded generator, `moment.random`, so the same seed slips the same, and a step on ground that can't slip draws nothing. Nothing is refilled: a careful player still slips now and then, and the rope is what makes it a scare and not a fall. `ClimberView.slipChance` is the chance a metre of the last step had, nought standing; the picture wobbles the climber by it, and the coach warns of careless steps.
- **Standing still is safe but where a stance is hard** (#197). A climber who takes no step in a step stands, and draws a chance a second of slipping, divided by their sure-footedness, only on the front points, on a face of ice or on blue ice steeper than `footing.still.steepness`, where a crampon's flat foot no longer holds (`technique.flat-footing`): `footing.still.frontPoints`, `iceClimb.oneToolSlip` times on the axe alone and `poor` times on a pick that bites poorly; and on a ladder, `footing.still.bounce` for each unit of its bounce past `footing.still.bounceFrom`, what a careful crossing sets going on its own, so another's hurried steps onto it count. On a face, a climber whose rope holds them where they are, its reach letting them no higher, rests on it and draws nothing: a changeover there is safe while the old rope still holds them. Anywhere else standing never slips. `ClimberView.stillChance` is the last step's, nought on the move.
- **A jolt they can't prevent** (#197), a gust or a lashed ladder's twist (`Jolts`, [simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Jolts) or a climber passing them on their rope, slips them at its chance times `unsteadiness(id)`: nought off their feet, lowering on a rope or resting on one on a face, and where nothing slips; whole on a ladder, on the front points, on blue ice and on snow steep enough to slip on, `footing.braced` of that standing still, braced for it; divided by their sure-footedness. `upset(id, step)` slips them from where they stand, a fall their rope holds. Passing: each step, a climber on their feet passed on the ground by another on a rope they share, within `footing.passing.near`, the one who moved the less, is jolted at `footing.passing.slip`; `passerOf(id, reach)` says who is coming past, for the coach.
- **The rope's cost is in the handling, not the walking** (#197). A climber walks at their technique's share of the speed on the ground, clipped or not, and never past the fastest walk. A clip takes the typical client's hands `clip` seconds, standing, their technique's share of it (`technique.clip-time`): its carabiner goes onto the rope only then (`ClimberView.clipping` says which is going on, a save keeps it), the next cowstail's clip after it, and no step meanwhile; `unclip` drops a clip going on, and a fall drops them all. `clip` nought clips at once, as the tests' mountains do.
- **The kit a climber checked before setting off** (`Kit`, #196) is on their layout (`ClimberLayout.kit`), all of it unless it says otherwise, and in their save. A crampon they didn't check comes loose on the first ladder they step onto once they've been on the move `footing.crampon.looseAfter` seconds; while it's loose they're `footing.crampon.loose` as sure-footed, each step that many times likelier to slip, and a fall then is written down as on a loose crampon (`Fall.looseCrampon`), for the debrief. The crampons tool, used on their feet on the ground, has them kneel `footing.crampon.refit` seconds and refit it: no step while they're down. A headlamp they didn't check dies `technique.dead-lamp-hours` after setting off, and until first light (`time.first-light`) they're `technique.dark-footing` as sure-footed: `Lamplight` stands between the body and the physics, cutting the sure-footedness the body hands on while the player is in the dark, with no state of its own (the clock and the kit are saved), and tells the rendering and the coach whose lamp is lit. A bottle they didn't fill is the body's: they set off `body.unchecked-water` litres short ([body.md](body.md)).
- **Blue ice** is a layout's stretches of the ground (`SlopeLayout.ice`), bare of snow, where the snow lies level on the line and a slide meets `footing.iceFriction` in place of snow's friction: a slip on the flat slides a metre, one on a slope of ice slides on, over a lip if there's one below.
- **A fall is a body** under Verlet integration in `Slope.SUBSTEPS` sub-steps a step, starting at the walking speed. The ground pushes it back out, and friction takes from its slide what Coulomb's law gives, so a gentle slope stops it and a steep one doesn't. In the air it loses no speed: a climber hanging on a rope swings until the ground stops them.
- **A fall is over when the body lies still on ground they can stand on** for a whole step (`Slope.REST_SPEED`), and the climber stands where it lies: against a wall's face, only their rope holds them, and they hang. Its record (`Fall`: the step it began on, the drop, the top speed, the hardest landing and whether a rope held them) stays in the climber's view, for the rules above to read.
- **A landing is the body's speed into the ground** as it meets it (#190): nought sliding along a slope or down a wall's face, the whole of a drop through the air onto the floor below. Past `lethalLanding`, life size as the falls are, the fall kills them: an unroped fall into a crevasse does.
- **Hanging on their rope off the ground, a climber climbs back up it** on the jumar: a key that points up moves them at `climbBack` × stride × technique while they hang (`ClimberView.hanging`), if their first cowstail is on the rope and they carry a jumar (`Gear.jumar`). A carabiner alone climbs no rope: `clip first` while hanging puts the jumar on the rope that holds them, beside the carabiner. Free on the rope they go straight to where they slipped, a ladder's rung included; against the ice (`Climber.TOUCH`) they go along the ground's line, up the wall's face and over its lip, never through it, to the ground they came off or the lip their ladder stands on, and off a face, to its top, or to its foot when no rope of theirs reaches the top, one anchored below the face (#197). They're on their feet on the first ground they can walk off. A haul (#209) moves them the same way, jumar or not.
- **A dead end is no place to be left standing** (`Faces.deadEnd`): a run of walkable ground the route doesn't reach, with no ladder's end, no rope's anchor and neither end of the section on it, such as a ledge down a crevasse's wall or the snow at its foot. A climber their rope holds still hangs there (`hangs`), to climb back up; one with no rope stands there, alive, and gets out on their tools or by a partner's haul.
- **Ice too steep to walk is climbed on the tools** (`OnIce`). A `Face` is a stretch of the ground steeper than `steepest`, all rising or all falling along the ground; `Faces` finds them once, from the terrain. The `tool` action is the player's hand: `ice-axe` pointed within `iceClimb.snap` of a face swings a pick in there, if the place is between `lockOff` and `reach` above the climber's feet, or over the face's lip within their reach, and their feet are on that face, at its foot, or their body hangs against it. On the ice a key that points up walks the feet up until the pick is `lockOff` above them, and one that points down, down until it's `reach` above; the next pick takes them further, a pick planted over the lip takes them onto the ground at the top, and the foot puts them back on their feet. `crampons` pointed at a face from its lip steps down onto it, the axe planted `overLip` past the lip. They climb at `iceClimb.speed` × stride × technique, `oneTool` of it on the ice axe alone (`Gear.tools` < 2), as far as their ropes let them, and tire at `hang` of a stride's power even still.
- **A pick bites well or poorly**: poorly above `stretch` over the feet, at full stretch, or in a layout's `brittle` stretches of a face. Each metre on the ice slips at `iceClimb.slip`, `oneToolSlip` times likelier on the axe alone and `poor` times likelier on a pick that bites poorly, over the climber's sureness; on the axe alone each new swing may come off, at `swingSlip`, since no pick is in the ice while it swings. A slip off the ice is a fall like any other, held by the rope they're clipped to.
- **Down a step of ice on its rope** (`Lowering`): a climber at the lip of a face no ladder stands at, clipped to a fixed rope that runs down it (`Rope.runsDown`: one anchor at the lip's height, a cowstail below it at most, and the other lower than the face's middle; a rope across a crevasse runs down neither side), lowers themselves down it with a key that points down, at `rappel` × stride × technique, along the ground's line to its foot, where they're on their feet, as climbers go down the fixed lines through a figure-8 descender (`technique.down-fixed-ropes`); a key that points up takes them back up it on the jumar, at `climbBack`. Their hand slides the jumar down with them, and the rope through their descender holds a slip, so there's none; off the rope down it, nothing holds them, and they fall. It takes `iceClimb.hang` of a stride's power, all of it going back up. `ClimberView.lowering` says who's on one, a save keeps it, and `iceWall(id)` says `rope` at such a lip, for the coach.
- **What a climber carries of the gear** (`Gear`: the jumar, and how many ice tools, the ice axe the first) is on their layout (`ClimberLayout.gear`), and `SlopePhysics.equip` changes it as their pack does ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Packs). Without a jumar the first cowstail is a plain carabiner: it grips nothing and climbs no rope.
- **A hazard's knock is a fall too.** The physics is the objective hazards' ground (`HazardGround`, [simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Objective hazards): `climbers` and `along(id)` say who's where along the ground, and `knock(id, step)` throws a climber on their feet down the section, toward its start, at `knock` metres a second. Their rope holds them as in any fall, and a climber already falling falls on. `clipped(id)` says whether they're on a rope, and `kill(id, step)` knocks them down for good: a dead climber lies where they come to rest, takes no actions, and leaves `climbers`.
- **The body sets the stride** (`stride(id, share)`, [body.md](body.md) → The pace and the stride): a climber walks on their share of the walking power, and climbs ladders at the speed by steepness times their share; they slip at the chance times their speed against a whole stride's there, so a slower climber slips as often for each metre, not each second. A whole stride, 1, until the body sets it; at 0 the climber stands, and draws nothing. `climber(id).moving` says they took a step in the last step, which the body reads to tire those on the move and rest the others.
- **Self-arrest stops a slide early.** The ice axe (the `tool` action, `ice-axe`) driven into the ground within `axeReach` of a sliding body, at its height or above, ends the fall there while the body touched the ground in the last sub-step and is no faster than `arrestSpeed`: in the first moments of a slide, and never in the air. The fall is recorded as any other, and the climber stands where it stopped. Whether the body touched the ground is saved with the fall, so a resumed slide arrests as it would have.

## Ladders

- **A ladder joins two points of the ground** (`Ladder`), across a crevasse or up a wall, its ends laid by how far along the ground they are, `from` the one further back. Walking crosses on it, not on the ground between its ends: at its end, the ground under it is no way on, so nobody walks off the edge beside it.
- **A key moves a climber along a ladder when it points more along it than across**, toward the end it points to: left and right across a crevasse, up and down (W/S) up a wall, and a diagonal on either. A ladder's ends stop a step, as the ground's corners do, and a climber who reaches an end steps off onto the ground there.
- **On a ladder a climber goes at `ladderSpeed` and slips at `ladderSlipChance` a metre**, by its steepness the way they go. A slip is a fall past it, since a falling body meets only the ground: off a ladder over a crevasse, the climber falls in unless a rope holds them, as a Sherpa fell in 2012.
- **A ladder staged to break** (#209) has the rung it `breaks` at in its layout: the section stages one a climb from the seed (`Section.staged`), in the middle third of one of its crossings at least `hazards.training-ladder-length` long, for the players, its `trainees` (#212). A trainee's step past that rung sets it creaking, and a Sherpa crossing first leaves it whole, the end behind them the one to tear free; `ladderBreak.creak` seconds later it breaks and swings down into the crevasse, and everyone on it falls straight down from their rung, the fall written down as off a ladder that broke (`Fall.broke`). Clipped, the rope holds them, and they climb out along it to the lip nearer where they stood (`Falling.lip`), the jumar running up the rope as they climb and gripping only when they stop; unclipped, they fall to the crevasse's floor. Nobody steps onto a broken ladder (`Slope.ladderFrom`), and no Icefall Doctors come to fix it (#228): it hangs in the crevasse until two climbers who can haul, holding the haul within its reach of either of its ends, one at each lip or both at one and hauling nobody, haul it back up together (`Slope.raising`), at their strides' share of two whole strides' pace, `ladderBreak.reset` seconds at whole strides (`technique.ladder-reset`); they pull, as a haul does, and take no step. One alone can't lift it; let go, it stays where it came to. All the way back, it's lashed across and holds from then on. `ladderCondition(id)` says whether it's whole, creaking or broken, `ladderTorn(id)` which end tore free and `ladderRaised(id)` how far it's been hauled back, 0 to 1; a save keeps them.
- **Hauling a partner out** (#209): a climber holding the haul (`haul` held), on their feet on the ground, hauls the climber hanging whose way up (`SlopePhysics.liftPoint`) is nearest them within `haul.reach` metres, up it at `haul.speed` times their stride, and takes no step while they do, their whole power spent (`exertion` 1, so the body tires them). The one hauled comes up as fast, and faster still climbing back themselves; a fall they were hauled out of says so, and who hauled (`Fall.pulled`, `Fall.pulledBy`, #212). `ClimberView.hauling` says who's hauling now.
- **A ladder's line held taut** (#212): a climber holding the haul at one of a ladder's ends, within `haul.reach` of it, with nobody hanging to haul, steadies it: the steps on it set it bouncing at `footing.bounce.held` of what they would (`technique.ladder-bounce-held`), as a Sherpa holding the line from the far side keeps a lashed ladder from sinking and swaying, so a crossing slips less.
- **A ladder across a crevasse bounces** under the steps on it (#190): each metre walked on it sets it going by `footing.bounce.excite` for each metre of its length, it dies away by `footing.bounce.damp` a second, in straight steps that round the same everywhere, and each unit of it adds `footing.bounce.slip` to the chance a metre slips for whoever walks on it. A long one bounces hardest, two on it twice as hard, and a hurried step more than a careful one; one up a wall doesn't. `ladderBounce(id)` says how hard, and a save keeps it.

## The rope

- **A fixed rope is a chain of points** about `Rope.LINK_METRES` apart, between two anchors. A link can only be shorter than its share of the rope, so the rope sags, lies on the ground with friction and swings, and never stretches. Each point is tethered to both anchors by the rope between, so a long chain holds in a few rounds. The ropes move once a step, after the falling bodies.
- **A cowstail clips to the nearest rope within its reach**, on a climber on their feet, and `unclip` takes it off. Walking takes the climber only where every rope they're clipped to lets them.
- **The first cowstail ends in the jumar, the second in a carabiner**, as climbers rig them. The carabiner always runs along its rope. The jumar runs only while the climber's hand slides it (the `jumar` action, held) and they're on their feet; otherwise it grips the rope where it is, in a fall too.
- **The rope holds a climber by its length**: no further from its two anchors together than the rope and two cowstails, an ellipse around the anchors. That's a carabiner sliding on a rope without friction, and a heavy climber can't stretch it as they would a chain of light points. Each climber is held as if alone on the rope, and the rope follows them: the point nearest the carabiner is pulled to within a cowstail.
- **A jumar that grips holds the climber by the rope on each side of it**: no further from each anchor than the rope between and a cowstail, a circle around each. A slip on the jumar stops a cowstail and its share of the slack below it; on the carabiner alone, it slides down to the anchor below.
- **Ropes in a line share their anchors**: the next rope starts where one ends. A clipped climber is stopped past the anchor, where the next rope is the nearest, so `clip` takes it. The safe way on is the second cowstail onto the next rope before the first comes off the old one. Taking the last one off is allowed, and a slip after that isn't held.
- **A rope over a bulge holds a little low**, since the ellipse goes straight from anchor to anchor: anchors go at the edges, as on the mountain.
- **A rope that lies still waits.** Once no point of it moves faster than `Slope.REST_SPEED` in a step, it skips its steps until a climber clips to it, so the ropes of a whole section cost nothing until used.

## Numbers

- **The mountain's numbers are handed in** through `SlopeSettings` (conventions.md → Data, not literals): gravity, friction, the cowstail's reach, the walking cost by gradient, as `[gradient, value]` points, the steepest ground walked, the walking power, the fastest walk and the share of the climb's extra cost a stride pays, the chance a metre slips by steepness on the ground and the speed and chance on a ladder, as `[degrees, value]` points, the slips' other numbers (`FootingSettings`), the lethal landing and the climb back, the speed of a hazard's knock, and the fastest slide self-arrest stops and the ice axe's reach; and through the layout, the altitude of the section's ends. `SlopeFacts` (`src/content/slope-facts.ts`) reads them from the knowledge base's `technique.*` records: gravity, the snow's friction and the walking cost (Minetti et al. 2002, `technique.walking-cost-*`) are facts; the walking power, the fastest walk, the climb's ease, the ladder speeds, the slips' chances, the cowstail, the knock, self-arrest, the lethal landing and the climb back are coefficients fitted to the Icefall's hours, up and down, and the chronicle (`src/content/icefall.story.test.ts` holds the crossings to the sources' hours), with the guide's questions beside them (`technique.speed-by-steepness`, `technique.slips-by-steepness`). The records are life's numbers: `SlopeFacts` hands the physics the walking power, the fastest walk, the ladder speeds, the climb back, the ice climbing's and the rappel's speeds times `time.compression` over `route.drawn-scale`, three, the share of the climb's cost as it is, and the bounce's dying away times the same; the slips' chances are a metre's, as in life; gravity, the frictions, the cowstail, the knock, self-arrest and the lethal landing stay at life size, as the falls do. The ground walked runs from the steepest down to the steepest up, the Icefall's `route.icefall-steepness`; a ladder, from down a wall to across to up one.
- **A section's ground is a file of `content/route/`**, read by `Section` (`src/content/section.ts`, [rendering.md](rendering.md)): the Khumbu Icefall, `icefall.json`, is the first (#26). The file gives the ground and the snow on it (`layout.snow`: its waves, how far it rounds a break of slope, how far round a wall or a ladder it lies on the line and how far past that it takes to rise, how far round a camp it's trodden flat, where the climbs set off and arrive, and its seed, the game's own, not the sources'), the ropes, the ladders, across a crevasse, up a wall or down one, the steps of ice the route climbs with no ladder on the tools (`route.icefall-ice-steps`), each with its rope from its foot to a metre back from its lip, where the next starts, and the brittle ice across them (`layout.brittle`), the camps' stretches, the hazards' zones, who sets off, and the route's parts, end to end along the whole ground, so the picture knows where the moraine ends and the snow begins (#172); the knowledge base gives, by the file's ids, the camps' altitudes, the hour it sets off at, its hours and its length, which its test holds the drawn width to.
- **The engine's numbers are in the code**, as the core's step is: `Slope.SUBSTEPS`, `Slope.ITERATIONS`, `Slope.REST_SPEED`, `Rope.LINK_METRES` and `Snow.STEP`. No source states them; they're how finely the physics works the mountain out.
- **Steepness is a sine** (`Steepness`): `ofWay` and `ofGradient` divide, and `ofDegrees` sums a series of its own, since `Math.sin` rounds differently in each browser. A `SteepnessCurve` interpolates over the sine and holds its end values past its ends, and so does the walking cost.

## The logbook

`Logbook` (#194) is a rule of its own, run after the physics in each step: for each climber, the mountain's seconds unclipped on the route (from the moraine's end to the camp the section ends at), and of them where a slip was a fall (on blue ice, on or by a ladder across a crevasse); under the seracs, and of that standing still; and the hours they arrived at the camp and died at. It reads the physics and the hazards and keeps its own numbers, which a save keeps; a save made before it can't be resumed, and starts the climb over. The debrief reads it ([rendering.md](rendering.md)).

## The same result everywhere

The physics uses the core's maths alone, and imports only itself and the core. `physics-purity.test.ts` checks both, with the core's checks (`src/simulation/testing/module-code.ts`), and `npm run typecheck` checks it with no DOM in scope, as it does the core. A save is plain JSON: the tests save, go through `JSON.stringify` and back, and resume exactly.

## Tests

The tests play the physics through `Expedition` (`testing/climb.ts`), on the made-up numbers and ground of `testing/test-mountain.ts`: no game code imports either. A test mountain is drawn at life size from `TestMountain.FOOT` unless the test says otherwise, and has no snow on its line unless the test lays `TestMountain.SNOW`. `Climb.under` goes on from a save on other numbers, a gust that makes every step a slip, say. The stories: `slope.story.test.ts`, two friends on a steep fixed rope, one of them careless; and `fixed-ropes.story.test.ts`, two Sherpas on a ladder over a crevasse, one unclipped, and two clients going up a line of fixed ropes on the jumar, one careless at the anchor; `hazards.story.test.ts`, two climbers under a serac in the afternoon, one clipped; a serac that cracks over two, the one near its edge walking clear; and crossing under it before dawn against the afternoon; and `icefall.story.test.ts`, a slip off a ladder over a crevasse, unclipped and clipped. `kit.story.test.ts` is the kit's (#196): a crampon not checked coming loose on the first ladder after an hour on the move, a climber on it less sure of their feet, a fall on it written down as such, and its refit on the ground and not on a ladder. `footing.story.test.ts` is the slips' (#208): blue ice slipping most steady climbers within 15 m and few careful ones, over seeds, and never the snow beside it; a slip on a ramp of ice sliding the unroped one over a crevasse's lip to their death and holding the clipped one to climb back up; a long ladder bouncing under a hurried crossing and under two, twice as likely to slip; a drop of 30 m killing where one of 3 doesn't; and the same seed slipping the same. The Icefall itself is crossed headless in `src/content/icefall.story.test.ts`, by `src/content/testing/crossing.ts`, a careful walker on the game's own numbers and ground, moving on the agents' `Footwork` as the Sherpa does ([agents.md](agents.md)); at a ladder down a wall it walks down onto it, and off its other end on the flat key, since a slanting one climbs an upright ladder from either end (#172); on blue ice and a ladder across it takes the rest step, unless it's told to keep its steady pace, and hanging after a slip it climbs back up and goes on its way (#208): on six seeds the careful one slips about once in two crossings and the steady one more. At a step of ice with no ladder it climbs it on its tools, a pick at a time as high as it bites well and clear of brittle ice, the last over the lip, and moves its cowstails onto the step's rope where the last rope holds it; coming down, it clips the rope down a step at its lip and lowers itself down it. `rappel.story.test.ts` is the rope down a step: a clipped climber stopping at its edge where one off the rope walks over it, lowering down it to its foot, back up it on the jumar and not on a carabiner, dropping off it unclipped, and going on from a save.
