# The slope physics

`src/physics/` moves the climbers over the ground and holds them on fixed ropes ([../../spec.md](../../spec.md) → Technical architecture: "our own simple physics, rope using Verlet integration"). It's a rule of the core, `SlopePhysics`, run inside its step ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Rules), and it keeps the core's rules for the same result everywhere. It follows [../conventions.md](../conventions.md) → Modules, Objects, and Time and chance; these are its own rules.

## The slope

- **The ground** (`Terrain`) is a line of points in metres, from left to right: `x` across and `y` up. `x` never goes back, so a wall is two points at the same `x`, and an overhang can't be drawn. Past its ends the ground goes on flat.
- **The snow lies on a section's line** (`Snow`, #168), a ground of its own with its points `Snow.STEP` apart across: the line's hollows filled and its edges rounded, and over it the swells and hummocks of a glacier's snow, tens of centimetres high over a few metres, from a seeded generator of its own (`SnowLayout`). By every wall, along every ladder and over the layout's level stretches, the camps' and a few metres round them, where the climbs set off, it lies on the line, and it rises smoothly to its full relief past them. It's measured along the line under it, so whatever a layout lays by how far along the ground stays where it was, and it turns only at the line's corners, so a step stops there alone. A climber walks its slope as any other, so its hummocks slow them and tire them by what their gradients cost; on the Icefall they're never steeper than the slips' `technique.slip-free-steepness`. A save keeps the line and the snow's layout, which lays the same snow again.
- **A scenario lays out a section** (`SlopePhysics.start`): its ground and the snow on it, its ropes by where their anchors are along the ground, its ladders by where their ends are, and the climbers standing on it. The physics works them out in the layout's order.
- **A section is drawn at 1/`route.drawn-scale` of life**, 12, its length and its height. The mountain's clock runs faster than that, `time.compression`, 36, and the climber goes as many times faster than in life over the drawn ground, three times, so they take a section's real hours by the clock ([simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Real time, #167). The layout gives the real altitude of its two ends (`SlopeLayout.altitude`), and `SectionScale` reads the altimeter from it: the real rise by the share of the section's width crossed, and the drawn ground's own ups and downs at life size, so a 10 m fall reads 10 m. `climber(id).altitude` is that reading. Each section's test holds its drawn rise to the scale (`SectionScale.ratio`).
- **A climber is a point at their feet.** On their feet, they're somewhere `along` the ground or on a ladder; falling, they're a body under gravity. `climber(id)` is how the rendering and the rules above see them, with the rope each cowstail is clipped to, where its carabiner is on it (`firstAt`, `secondAt`, the first the jumar) and whether the jumar is held; `ropes` and `ladders` name what's laid, and `rope(id)` and `ladder(id)` say where a rope or a ladder lies.

## Walking and falls

- **Walking goes by what the steepness ahead costs** (`Walking`, #150). `walkingCost` is the energy a metre of walking takes of each kilogram moved, measured by gradient, the rise over the run, as the physiologists' treadmills give it; past the steepest gradient measured, each metre of height costs what it costs there, up to `steepest`. A climber goes as fast as their stride's share of `walkingPower` pays for, and no faster than `fastestWalk`: up a slope the power holds them back, down one and on the flat their footing does. Up a slope a stride pays the level's cost and `uphillCostShare` of the climb's extra over it, a playability tuning that eases the climb (#186): a slope never goes faster than the level, and the steeper it is the more it's eased. A step stops at a corner, so the next one takes the new ground's speed. Ground steeper up than `steepest` stops the climber; ground steeper down is a fall.
- **A step's exertion is the share of the stride's power it takes**: all of it where the power holds the climber back and on a ladder, less where the fastest walk does, and nought still. `climber(id).exertion` says what the last step took, which the body reads to tire them by it ([body.md](body.md) → Fatigue), and a save keeps it.
- **A slip is a draw**, of `slipChance` per second scaled to the step, made only where that chance isn't zero: walking on flat ground draws nothing, and a change in where the physics draws changes every draw after it (simulation-core.md → The same result everywhere).
- **A fall is a body** under Verlet integration in `Slope.SUBSTEPS` sub-steps a step, starting at the walking speed. The ground pushes it back out, and friction takes from its slide what Coulomb's law gives, so a gentle slope stops it and a steep one doesn't. In the air it loses no speed: a climber hanging on a rope swings until the ground stops them.
- **A fall is over when the body lies still on the ground** for a whole step (`Slope.REST_SPEED`), and the climber stands where it lies. Its record (`Fall`: the step it began on, the drop and the top speed) stays in the climber's view, for the rules above to read.
- **A hazard's knock is a fall too.** The physics is the objective hazards' ground (`HazardGround`, [simulation-core.md](simulation-core.md) → Objective hazards): `climbers` and `along(id)` say who's where along the ground, and `knock(id, step)` throws a climber on their feet down the section, toward its start, at `knock` metres a second. Their rope holds them as in any fall, and a climber already falling falls on. `clipped(id)` says whether they're on a rope, and `kill(id, step)` knocks them down for good: a dead climber lies where they come to rest, takes no actions, and leaves `climbers`.
- **The body sets the stride** (`stride(id, share)`, [body.md](body.md) → The pace and the stride): a climber walks on their share of the walking power, and climbs ladders at the speed by steepness times their share; they slip at the chance times their speed against a whole stride's there, so a slower climber slips as often for each metre, not each second. A whole stride, 1, until the body sets it; at 0 the climber stands, and draws nothing. `climber(id).moving` says they took a step in the last step, which the body reads to tire those on the move and rest the others.
- **Self-arrest stops a slide early.** The ice axe (the `tool` action, `ice-axe`) driven into the ground within `axeReach` of a sliding body, at its height or above, ends the fall there while the body touched the ground in the last sub-step and is no faster than `arrestSpeed`: in the first moments of a slide, and never in the air. The fall is recorded as any other, and the climber stands where it stopped. Whether the body touched the ground is saved with the fall, so a resumed slide arrests as it would have.

## Ladders

- **A ladder joins two points of the ground** (`Ladder`), across a crevasse or up a wall, its ends laid by how far along the ground they are, `from` the one further back. Walking crosses on it, not on the ground between its ends: at its end, the ground under it is no way on, so nobody walks off the edge beside it.
- **A key moves a climber along a ladder when it points more along it than across**, toward the end it points to: left and right across a crevasse, up and down (W/S) up a wall, and a diagonal on either. A ladder's ends stop a step, as the ground's corners do, and a climber who reaches an end steps off onto the ground there.
- **On a ladder a climber goes at `ladderSpeed` and slips at `ladderSlipChance`**, by its steepness the way they go. A slip is a fall past it, since a falling body meets only the ground: off a ladder over a crevasse, the climber falls in unless a rope holds them, as a Sherpa fell in 2012.

## The rope

- **A fixed rope is a chain of points** about `Rope.LINK_METRES` apart, between two anchors. A link can only be shorter than its share of the rope, so the rope sags, lies on the ground with friction and swings, and never stretches. Each point is tethered to both anchors by the rope between, so a long chain holds in a few rounds. The ropes move once a step, after the falling bodies.
- **A cowstail clips to the nearest rope within its reach**, on a climber on their feet, and `unclip` takes it off. Walking takes the climber only where every rope they're clipped to lets them.
- **The first cowstail ends in the jumar, the second in a carabiner**, as climbers rig them. The carabiner always runs along its rope. The jumar runs only while the climber's hand slides it (the `jumar` action, held) and they're on their feet; otherwise it grips the rope where it is, in a fall too.
- **The rope holds a climber by its length**: no further from its two anchors together than the rope and two cowstails, an ellipse around the anchors. That's a carabiner sliding on a rope without friction, and a heavy climber can't stretch it as they would a chain of light points. Each climber is held as if alone on the rope, and the rope follows them: the point nearest the carabiner is pulled to within a cowstail.
- **A jumar that grips holds the climber by the rope on each side of it**: no further from each anchor than the rope between and a cowstail, a circle around each. A slip on the jumar stops a cowstail and its share of the slack below it; on the carabiner alone, it slides down to the anchor below.
- **Ropes in a line share their anchors**: the next rope starts where one ends. A clipped climber is stopped past the anchor, where the next rope is the nearest, so `clip` takes it. The safe way on is the second cowstail onto the next rope before the first comes off the old one. Taking the last one off is allowed, and a slip after that isn't held.
- **A rope over a bulge holds a little low**, since the ellipse goes straight from anchor to anchor: anchors go at the edges, as on the mountain.
- **A rope that lies still waits.** Once no point of it moves faster than `Slope.REST_SPEED` in a step, it skips its steps until a climber clips to it, so the ropes of a whole section cost nothing until used.

## Numbers

- **The mountain's numbers are handed in** through `SlopeSettings` (conventions.md → Data, not literals): gravity, friction, the cowstail's reach, the walking cost by gradient, as `[gradient, value]` points, the steepest ground walked, the walking power, the fastest walk and the share of the climb's extra cost a stride pays, the slip chance by steepness on the ground and the speed and slip chance on a ladder, as `[degrees, value]` points, the speed of a hazard's knock, and the fastest slide self-arrest stops and the ice axe's reach; and through the layout, the altitude of the section's ends. `SlopeFacts` (`src/content/slope-facts.ts`) reads them from the knowledge base's `technique.*` records: gravity, the snow's friction and the walking cost (Minetti et al. 2002, `technique.walking-cost-*`) are facts; the walking power, the fastest walk, the climb's ease, the ladder speeds, the slips, the cowstail, the knock and self-arrest are coefficients fitted to the Icefall's hours, up and down, and the chronicle (`src/content/icefall.story.test.ts` holds the crossings to the sources' hours), with the guide's questions beside them (`technique.speed-by-steepness`, `technique.slips-by-steepness`). The records are life's numbers: `SlopeFacts` hands the physics the walking power, the fastest walk and the ladder speeds times `time.compression` over `route.drawn-scale`, three, the share of the climb's cost as it is, and the slips' chances a second times the same, so a climber slips as often a metre as in life; gravity, the friction, the cowstail, the knock and self-arrest stay at life size, as the falls do. The ground walked runs from the steepest down to the steepest up, the Icefall's `route.icefall-steepness`; a ladder, from down a wall to across to up one.
- **A section's ground is a file of `content/route/`**, read by `Section` (`src/content/section.ts`, [rendering.md](rendering.md)): the Khumbu Icefall, `icefall.json`, is the first (#26). The file gives the ground and the snow on it (`layout.snow`: its waves, how far it rounds a break of slope, how far round a wall or a ladder it lies on the line and how far past that it takes to rise, how far round a camp it's trodden flat, where the climbs set off and arrive, and its seed, the game's own, not the sources'), the ropes, the ladders, across a crevasse, up a wall or down one, the camps' stretches, the hazards' zones, who sets off, and the route's parts, end to end along the whole ground, so the picture knows where the moraine ends and the snow begins (#172); the knowledge base gives, by the file's ids, the camps' altitudes, the hour it sets off at, its hours and its length, which its test holds the drawn width to.
- **The engine's numbers are in the code**, as the core's step is: `Slope.SUBSTEPS`, `Slope.ITERATIONS`, `Slope.REST_SPEED`, `Rope.LINK_METRES` and `Snow.STEP`. No source states them; they're how finely the physics works the mountain out.
- **Steepness is a sine** (`Steepness`): `ofWay` and `ofGradient` divide, and `ofDegrees` sums a series of its own, since `Math.sin` rounds differently in each browser. A `SteepnessCurve` interpolates over the sine and holds its end values past its ends, and so does the walking cost.

## The same result everywhere

The physics uses the core's maths alone, and imports only itself and the core. `physics-purity.test.ts` checks both, with the core's checks (`src/simulation/testing/module-code.ts`), and `npm run typecheck` checks it with no DOM in scope, as it does the core. A save is plain JSON: the tests save, go through `JSON.stringify` and back, and resume exactly.

## Tests

The tests play the physics through `Expedition` (`testing/climb.ts`), on the made-up numbers and ground of `testing/test-mountain.ts`: no game code imports either. A test mountain is drawn at life size from `TestMountain.FOOT` unless the test says otherwise, and has no snow on its line unless the test lays `TestMountain.SNOW`. `Climb.under` goes on from a save on other numbers, a gust that makes every step a slip, say. The stories: `slope.story.test.ts`, two friends on a steep fixed rope, one of them careless; and `fixed-ropes.story.test.ts`, two Sherpas on a ladder over a crevasse, one unclipped, and two clients going up a line of fixed ropes on the jumar, one careless at the anchor; `hazards.story.test.ts`, two climbers under a serac in the afternoon, one clipped; a serac that cracks over two, the one near its edge walking clear; and crossing under it before dawn against the afternoon; and `icefall.story.test.ts`, a slip off a ladder over a crevasse, unclipped and clipped. The Icefall itself is crossed headless in `src/content/icefall.story.test.ts`, by `src/content/testing/crossing.ts`, a careful walker on the game's own numbers and ground; at a ladder down a wall it walks down onto it, and off its other end on the flat key, since a slanting one climbs an upright ladder from either end (#172).
